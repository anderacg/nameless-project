# A Name on the Road

Proposed opening story for Ashmark, 2026-10-23. Design only; not implemented.

## Purpose

Give a new player an immediate answer to "what should I do?" and a small reason to care. In roughly 15-25 minutes of first-time play, the player takes a first contract, prepares for travel, meets people outside a settlement, commands a small band or negotiates, and earns a local reputation. That duration is a playtest target.

The story is about becoming someone people can rely on. Its stakes are one missing cart and a village waiting for food. It suits every background without inventing a shared family tragedy, military service, or allegiance. Finishing it earns an introduction and more opportunities, not a kingdom or a unique legendary item.

## Premise and cast

A cart carrying grain from the starting settlement to a nearby village has not arrived. The guards are keeping watch over the settlement and cannot spare a search party for a cart that might merely have broken a wheel. A local roadkeeper offers the newcomer a small paid errand: take a note to the village and find out what happened.

Four small roles are enough: the roadkeeper who hires you, a surviving carter, the village head who needs the grain, and the leader of three deserters who took it. Their appearance and names fit the local population; no existing lord or companion is required.

Opening line:

> "Looking for work? A grain cart left for {village} yesterday. No cart, no word. Take this note to the village head and find out what happened. I'll pay for the road."

## The first minute

The player begins at their normal starting settlement's gate. A brief opening page gives one sentence of context and two actions: **Look for work** or **Find my own way**. Closing it leaves the same invitation available at the settlement.

Choosing work pins **Enter {home} and speak to the roadkeeper**. Entering opens the relevant story conversation through the settlement's Jobs area, without requiring the player to search its other tabs. The roadkeeper explains the contract, advance, and destination before acceptance.

Choosing independence hides the invitation and gives no penalty. The player can find the roadkeeper later. The opening story is offered once per campaign, outside the random job refresh and encounter rolls.

## Quest sequence

| Part | What happens | Current objective | What the player learns |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Work for a stranger | Accept the roadkeeper's errand, receive a note and travel advance, and prepare if desired. | Take the note to the head of {village}. | Settlement navigation, supplies, recruitment, and following a map marker. |
| 2. The cart that never came | On the short route, discover the damaged cart and its surviving carter. The grain was taken, not lost. | Investigate the cart; then speak to the village head. | Recognizing and approaching a map encounter, reading choices, and following new information. |
| 3. People are waiting | Deliver the note. The village head describes the missing supplies and points out where the deserters were seen. Ask about local recruits or supplies if needed. | Recover the village's grain from the deserters. | Local reputation, checking enemy strength, and preparing a small band. |
| 4. A price for the grain | Confront three poorly equipped deserters. Fight for the grain or negotiate its return for part of your contract payment. | Bring the recovered grain to {village}. | Basic command and combat, or resolving trouble through a deal. |
| 5. A name remembered | Return the supplies, receive payment and thanks, and choose a useful lead into the wider campaign. | Choose where to go next. | Handing in work, rewards, and the game's continuing opportunities. |

### 1. Work for a stranger

The note is recorded as a bound quest item using the existing quest-item rules. Its destination is a nearby friendly village, selected by reachable travel distance rather than straight-line distance. This opening needs a short courier variant; the normal courier's longer destination range should not dictate its route.

Draft advance: **40 gold**, some local food and drink sufficient for the planned journey, and **+5 reputation at home** as the roadkeeper publicly vouches for the errand. Validate that the advance plus every background's starting purse covers two available tier-1 recruits while retaining travel supplies. Adjust these draft values after checking current prices and supply consumption.

Two optional prompts appear only where useful: **Hire a few locals** and **Check food and drink**. Players with troops or supplies already satisfy those suggestions. They may leave alone; opening a shop or hiring an exact number is never a progression requirement.

Reserve a small, one-time allocation of eligible local volunteers so a new player is not taught recruitment through an empty recruitment screen. Use normal recruitment prices, standing, capacity, and army-group rules. Do not grant recruits repeatedly by reopening the conversation.

### 2. The cart that never came

Place this encounter on a reachable, visible approach to the village. It is guaranteed by the story and uses its own placement trigger, independent of random travel encounters and their cooldowns.

The carter is shaken and bruised but can talk. Asking what happened always reveals the essential information. Sharing food gives an optional small kindness response; Medicine or Scouting adds detail without being necessary.

> "Three of them. Soldiers once, by their coats. Took the sacks and left the cart. Said hungry men had as much right to grain as anyone."

The encounter updates the journal to explain the theft. It does not force the player into a fight or require an escort passenger system.

If the player misses the cart, the village head provides the essential information and the story continues. Investigation remains a short optional detour rather than a mandatory backtrack. Visiting the village first therefore works correctly.

### 3. People are waiting

The village head shows the practical consequence: its reserves are low and the shipment was already paid for. A short line and the settlement scene are enough; no new famine simulation is required.

Delivering the note pays **20 gold** and grants **+5 village reputation**. This provides a useful early success and, with the existing same-race trust bonus, reaches the current tier-1 recruitment threshold for a matching population. Destination selection must account for that trust; do not assume every village shares the player's race.

The village head marks one specific deserter band nearby. Show that there are three poorly equipped fighters before the player commits. Do not use an arbitrary existing bandit camp: its garrison and reinforcements could overwhelm a beginner.

The proposed recovery payment is **80 gold**, explained before departure. A short preparation conversation is available here too, including why local volunteers will now follow the player. Recruitment remains optional.

### 4. A price for the grain

The deserters are hungry former soldiers who chose robbery. Their circumstances give the player a decision without pretending the theft has no victim.

> "Orders stopped. Pay stopped. Hunger didn't. Tell your roadkeeper we can eat his promises when he starts sending them."

Always offer these choices:

- **Return the grain, and I'll pay you from the contract.** The deserters accept 40 gold held back from the player's promised 80-gold recovery payment. This is a written split of the existing contract, not an immediate payment that can leave the player unable to proceed. The village receives all its grain; the player receives 40 gold at hand-in. No skill roll is required.
- **The grain goes back. Stand aside or fight.** Opens the encounter's battle confirmation with enemy numbers and the option to leave. Winning recovers the grain; normal retreat or defeat leaves a recovery path.
- **Leave for now.** Keeps the objective and location available. No automatic abandonment or reputation penalty.

The band has three low-tier fighters appropriate to the region, with no elite leader, ranged burst, monsters, or reinforcing camp. Its composition is fixed when the opening is created; it does not grow with elapsed campaign days. A player who returns much stronger may earn a surrender through the existing parley rules, receiving the normal recovery payment.

For a first manual battle, present only the next useful command: select a group, move it, then attack. Use the game's real controls and touch equivalents. Keep further formation and ability guidance optional. The deal route completes the whole opening without a battle; its final leads can offer a later bounty for players who want combat practice.

Represent the recovered shipment as bound quest cargo with a short explanation that the village sends someone to collect the sacks once the band releases them. The player carries the release note or proof, not a cartload that exceeds their bag capacity. Mark the shipment released only after a successful resolution, never merely because the fight started.

### 5. A name remembered

The player returns to the village, hands in the proof, and receives **80 gold after combat/surrender or 40 gold after the deal**, **+8 village reputation**, a modest XP reward, and a written introduction. Both successful routes receive the same reputation: the village asked for its grain, and got it.

The village head thanks the player using the actual outcome. The carter is mentioned again so the first encounter has a visible consequence.

> "Yesterday you were a stranger on the road. Today, people here know your name. If you're looking for more work, I'll put my word behind you."

Finish at the village; do not require another journey back to the starting settlement. Its head can settle the roadkeeper's contract under the authority of the opening note.

The introduction reveals three concrete, currently valid leads:

- **More local work:** open the village's real Jobs offers.
- **A merchant's living:** mark a reachable market with a useful current trade lead. Show where to go; do not promise a fixed profit.
- **A lord's service:** mark a reachable friendly lord or their holding, with a personal introduction. This opens a conversation, not automatic faction membership or guaranteed employment.

Only the lead the player chooses is tracked. The others remain in the completed story's journal entry. These destinations can refresh if the world changes. This closes the small main story and hands the player an immediate next direction.

## Guidance and recovery rules

- Show one compact tracked objective with a destination and a **Show on map** action. Put the other steps and optional preparation advice in the journal. Update it immediately after a relevant event.
- Keep opening dialogue short: usually two or three sentences and clear action labels. Offer more questions instead of front-loading world lore.
- Do not require a talent point, a particular weapon, a purchase, a healing skill, or a minimum army size. Players should understand an activity's value before deciding whether to use it.
- No opening deadline. The player can explore, recover, or earn money and return. They can untrack the story without abandoning it or losing reputation.
- A surviving player who loses the battle can retreat, replace losses, retry, or take the deal. Hero death follows the campaign's normal rules; the story grants no hidden resurrection.
- Every stage, advance, reward, recruit reservation, and band resolution must survive save/load and occur at most once. Repeated dialogue cannot produce extra gold, grain, or troops.
- Offer the opening before random jobs can fill the quest list. Keep only its current quest active. If the next step cannot start because the list is full, retain a visible pending step and allow the player to resume it later.
- Select the village on the same reachable land network, with a short route suitable for the starting climate's travel needs. Prefer the player's local population and a peaceful destination. If none fits, bind the village-head role to a nearby friendly holding and adapt the wording.
- Keep the cart and band clear of occupied tiles, water, settlement gates, and major hostile sites. Avoid placing a beginner's mandatory approach through a known stronger enemy.
- Bind targets by identity. The opening band must not be silently removed by ordinary offscreen cleanup or treated as an unrelated bounty target. If another legitimate world action resolves it, reconcile the shipment and advance the story instead of leaving a missing objective.
- If war or capture makes an objective inaccessible, provide an alternate friendly contact or route. An impossible story stage should pause visibly or rebind safely rather than remain a marker behind closed hostile gates.
- New campaigns receive the invitation. Existing campaigns do not suddenly open a prologue window; the roadkeeper may offer it explicitly if the player wants it.

## Implementation scope

Reuse the existing Thread, Quest, Choice, Outcome, map markers, settlement pages, recruitment service, and battle/parley systems. Add one opening thread, a short courier/delivery quest variant, a fixed-target recovery quest, the carter and deserter encounter kinds, and the compact objective tracker. Keep tunables and story-kind declarations with the project's data/story definitions rather than scattering special cases across UI files.

The existing encounter-end issue must be corrected first: an encounter disappearing or handing off to a battle does not consistently notify a waiting thread. The recovery quest must listen to the actual result, not consider battle entry a victory. Also handle a full quest list without dropping the next step.

Suggested build order:

1. Reliable lifecycle and pending-step handling; opening invitation, roadkeeper conversation, and tracker.
2. Reachable destination selection, note delivery, preparation advice, and guaranteed cart encounter.
3. Village conversation, fixed deserter band, battle/deal branches, and recovery hand-in.
4. Ending leads, save/load coverage, and desktop/touch presentation checks.

Do not add passenger escorts, a companion, a large dungeon, an invasion, a new faction, or a long conspiracy to this first version. The existing wounded-traveller chain can follow as a separate story after this opening establishes the basic campaign loop.

## Acceptance checks

- A first-time player sees a specific next destination within the first minute and knows why they are going there.
- Every race/background has an affordable preparation path and a reachable opening route on generated worlds, including sparse holdings and harsh climates.
- A player can finish with combat or a guaranteed deal, without meeting a skill threshold.
- Skipping the cart, arriving early, leaving a conversation, retreating, returning with a stronger army, or filling the quest list does not strand the story.
- Save/load at every stage preserves the exact target, payment split, supplies released, and next objective; rewards cannot repeat.
- Existing quests and random encounters continue to function alongside the opening.
- Desktop and phone landscape show one readable objective and usable map, conversation, and battle guidance.
- The ending gives an actionable next lead and leaves the player free to choose another.
