# PX engine

Reusable procedural pixel-art generators. They are plain scripts with no build step, so they work when the page is opened straight from disk. Everything lives on `window.PX`.

| File | What it gives |
|---|---|
| `pixel.js` | `PX.rng`, `PX.dice`, `PX.hash` (seeded randomness); `PX.shade`, `PX.mix` (colour); `PX.canvas`, `PX.paint` (rect, px, poly, line); `PX.outline` (the dark rim characters and buildings share); `PX.MAT` (the medieval palette, by material) |
| `buildings.js` | `PX.building({type, style, seed, banner, facing, deep})` returns `{img, shadow, w, h, ax, ay, tiles, facing, deep}` for a 16 px tile map; `PX.BUILDING_TYPES` lists the types |
| `pieces.js` | `PX.prop({type})`: 8×8 yard props. `PX.pier` (`PX.boat` is legacy: the game moors the sailing ships' sprites, `ships.js` `shipSprite({moored:true})`), and `PX.camp({kind, seed})`: a bandit camp is one or two tents and a fire on 2×2 tiles; a hideout is the same ringed by barricades of spiked logs (4×4); travellers have two tents, a fire and their wagon (3×2). It returns `flag` (the foot of a flag pole, for the game's own flag) and `fire` (the foot of the flame). `PX.flame(frame)`: the flame, frames 0–3, for the game to animate. There are no wall pieces: a holding's walls are its centre's fortification (`spec.fort`) |
| `ground.js` | `PX.battleGround({C,R,cs,kind,lvl,climate,seed,base})`: a battle's terrain (doc/TERRAIN_DESIGN.md §4: terraces 0–2 as raised plateaus, their climbable edges smooth rises, woods of mixed species on the flat or on hills, lone trees and copses, marsh, small details by climate; desert bare, its marsh an oasis; rivers: deep water shaded by depth with current lines, banks, reeds and stones, fords light over gravel with stepping stones and foam, wooden or stone bridges; frost: ice fords, shelves and floes; swamp: murky) painted once over the climate's arena canvas `base`, the field's size; `PX.battleWater(g,ground,t,view)`: a river's motion each frame over its ground (glints and current down the deep, riffles on fords, sparkles and drifting ice in the frost; seeded at paint time into `ground.water`, drawn from the time `t`, culled to `view`); `PX.battleGroundMini(spec,ground)`: the small map, a pixel a cell; standing props (phase 3): the trees as 32-px sprites (`PX.battleTree(climate,species,variant)`: oak, birch, fir, snowy pine, bare birch, dead, willow, palm; 3 variants each) and the plateaus' south cliff faces cut into sections are returned as `ground.props` (sorted by their feet: `{dk:1|2, x, y, s, dx, dy, box}`) for the game to draw in depth order with its soldiers, their shadows and roots painted on the ground; `PX.battleProps(g,ground,view)` draws them over the ground alone; `PX.GROUND_PAL` the palettes by climate (`rock`: each climate's mountain and cliff rock). `PX.battleObstacle(kind,clim,v)`: the obstacles of a field without terrain (`tree`: a `battleTree` of the climate's; `rock`: a boulder of its rock; `cactus`: a saguaro or a barrel; 6 looks), `{s, ax, ay, box, g, gx, gy}`: the standing sprite (drawn at the point minus `ax, ay`, depth-sorted) and what lies on the ground under it (shadow, roots, scree); `PX.battleDetails(g,W,H,clim,seed)`: the plain field's small things by climate (the game's arena); `PX.battleShadow(w)`: the soft ground shadow; `PX.smallTree(clim,species,v)`: the 16-px trees (a town's streets). Seeded, never `Math.random`. **Phase 4:** kind 6 is rock: each mass of rock cells is a mountain drawn with real height (`spec.rockH`, a cell's height in levels of `PX.ROCK_PX` = 14 px, or `spec.rockPx`; a rock cell without one is 3): a massif of faceted, ridged peaks, a lone spire of 1–2 cells a needle; grey granite with grass tufts (grass), snow-capped (frost), banded sandstone buttes and mesas (desert), dark mossy rock (swamp); lit from the upper left, cast shadows and scree on the ground. Its pixels are cut into props `{dk:3, …}` like the faces, so soldiers sort and fade against it. `spec.wall` (edge bits E 1, S 2, W 4, N 8) marks sheer cliffs: a terrace edge with a bit on either side is grey rock, 12 px a level, cracked, a bright rim, a long shadow, a thick dark north lip; an edge with none (every edge with no `spec.wall`) is a smooth rise (the ground's cover shaded in soft steps to its foot, grassy, earthy or stony by stretches; never a prop); a slope in a ring with cliffs is a ramp, purely natural: the cliff breaks and the hillside slopes down through the gap (its ground shaded in four steps, earth showing through, tufts, scree at its foot, a rock shoulder each side). Roads and paths (`spec.ways`) are worn cart tracks (faint wandering ruts, a patchy crown, soft edges) and trodden strips; trees keep their crowns off its line (a gap in the canopy). `ground.under`: per-cell ground colours where the rock stands over a cell, for the small map, which colours rock cells as rock and, with walls, darkens a cliff's contour |
| `beasts.js` | `PX.beast(kind, {v, pose, team})`: the battle's beasts and monsters (`PX.BEAST_KINDS`: wolf, boar, bear, tiger, griffin, spider, scorpion, slime, troll, wyrm, dragon) in the people figures' style: side-on facing right, hue-shifted four-tone ramps lit from the top left, a dark navy outline round the silhouette only; a canvas per kind (the dragon fills a 64 cell, the troll and the wyrm 32, the slime 16, the rest 32) with the feet on the row above its bottom one; `v` one of three seeded variants (coat, shade, marks); `pose` as the game's people (0-1 standing, 2-5 walking, 15-18 running, 6-8 the blow, 9-11 the dragon's breath, `PX.BEAST_DEAD` lying dead); `team` 0 a collar or band in the side's blue. `c.fh` the standing figure's height. `PX.beastMap(kind, f)` the world map's figure (the dragon flying). `PX.ramp(colour)` the four-tone ramp. Never `Math.random` |
| `worldnature.js` | the world map's nature on 16-px tiles, in the engine's style (hue-shifted 4-tone ramps, lit from the upper left, a navy `#1a1c26` outline round silhouettes only, a ground shadow to the lower right; seeded, never `Math.random`). Sprites in power-of-two boxes, each a canvas with `ax, ay` (its foot), `box`, `flat`, and for the painter `a` (1 body, 2 outline), `rgb`, `sh` (its shadow): `PX.worldTree(clim,sp,v,dim)` a 16-px tree (`PX.WORLD_SPECIES`: grass oak, birch, pine; frost snowy pine, fir; swamp willow, dead, mossy oak; desert palm), 4 shapes, `dim` one deep in a wood; `PX.worldDecor(clim,kind,v)` the small things in 8 or 16 boxes (`PX.WORLD_DECOR`: bushes, stones, rocks, cacti, scrub, shrubs, stumps standing; tufts, flowers, drifts, ripples, reeds, moss, pools, bones, mushrooms, oasis green, lily pads, floes lying flat, unoutlined); `PX.worldHill(clim,v,opts)` a rise, a height bump `z` in a 32 box (`opts.edge` kept for cliffs, a later feature); `PX.worldPeak(clim,v,n)` a mountain alone in a 16, 32 or 64 box, drawn as the ranges are. `PX.worldRange(o)`: mountain ranges as the battle's (`ground.js` rock): each mass of rock tiles a height field (fluted cones as high as a tile lies deep in its range, buttes in steps in the desert, a spire if it is one or two tiles) drawn in projection, front to back a column at a time, lit from the left, its own and a cast shadow, granite, snow, sandstone strata or moss by climate, outlined round its silhouette and contoured along its crests; never rising over kept-clear ground. `PX.worldNature(spec)`: all of it over a painted map in depth order (`{D, W, H, C, R, ts, kind, clim, clear, seed, touch}`: the map's RGBA pixels, its tiles' kinds `PX.WN_KIND` (the game's codes) and climates, a `PX.TileMask` or a byte a pixel where nothing may stand): a wood's floor darkened and its trees as one mass (outlined round its edge only, deeper trees dimmer, the edge broken by a tree or a bush outside it), hills shaded as one ground from their rises, the ranges, small things on the open, palms only by a small water in the desert, pads and floes on water; nothing on clear ground or water; returns its counts and timings. A big map is painted a window at a time: `PX.worldNaturePlan(spec)` once (what is global, a byte or so a tile), then per window `{x, y, w, h, inner}` `PX.worldNaturePaint(plan, win, D, TO)` or its generator `PX.worldNatureSteps` (sliced), painted `PX.WN_APRON` px larger than what is kept, the windows stitched equal to `PX.worldNature`'s picture; `PX.TileMask(C, R, ts)` is a bit a pixel kept by tile (`at`, `fill`, `paint`, `copy`, `bytes`; `PX.TileMask.of` from a byte a pixel) |
| `ground-fixture.js` | previews only (not loaded by the game): `PX.groundFixture({recipe,C,R,seed})`, stand-in terrain in the §4 shape (`hills`, `forest`, `marsh`, `mixed`, `hillwoods`, `oasis`; rivers `river`, `bridge`, `fords`, `bridgehills`, `stream`, `frozen`; phase 4 with `rockH` and `wall`: `spires` (a massif, lone spires, a crag), `plateau` (plateaus ringed by one-way cliffs, one ramp each: south, west, north), `cliffs` (a hill of two-way cliffs beside one of slopes), `mountriver` (a massif on a river's bank, spires)) |
| `gallery.html` | a preview of every building type for each people across seeds; it takes the URL options `?z=4&n=8&only=keep`; `?view=props` shows the props and camps; `?only=sites` the sites; `?view=castles` the keeps, citadels, villages and halls; `?view=ground` the battlefield ground with its standing trees and cliff faces, each recipe in each climate (`&only=hills`, `&only=rivers`, `&only=rock`, `&c=frost`, `&z=2`, `&seed=`); `?view=depth` stand-in soldiers before and behind a spire, the massif and a cliff, without and with the fade over your own (`&c=`); `?view=bench` the painting cost and a frame's prop cost on the largest field; `?view=beasts` every beast and monster beside a stand-in man for scale, each pose, dead, its variants, on your side and on the map (`&z=`, `&only=wolf,dragon`, `&v=`; `&fit=1` how far each kind's poses reach); `?view=nature` the world map's nature by climate (trees, deep trees, small things, hills, peaks 16, 32, 64) and a stand-in map each painted by `PX.worldNature` (`&c=`, `&z=`, `&mz=` its zoom, `&seed=`) |

## Building conventions
- **Cells (the sizing rule):**
  - Every building fills a square cell of 16, 32 or 64 px: 1×1, 2×2 or 4×4 tiles. Houses, halls, trades and work places are 16; the treehouse, spire, watchtower, gatehouse and village 32; the keep and citadel 64. Props are 8.
  - The whole picture, roof, chimney and banner included, fits inside its cell, and its footprint on the map is the same square. So placed buildings never overlap.
  - `img` is `cell×cell` with `ax` 0 and `ay` = cell. Draw it at `tileX*16`, `(tileY+1)*16 - cell`, where (tileX, tileY) is the cell's bottom-left tile.
  - The fit step leaves things off or makes the building smaller until it fits. The game lays its holdings out from these pieces (see the game's `js/world/lots.js`).
- **Camera:** one camera for everything. A box seen from the front, one side receding back at half depth.
- **Top-down castles:**
  - The keep (castle centre, 64), the citadel (town centre, 64) and the gatehouse (32) are drawn straight on and symmetric, but seen from above, as in a top-down game.
  - The `village` (village centre, 32) is seen from above too: its people's hall (`spec.style`: longhouse, stonehall, elfhall or hidehall; `PX.VILLAGE_HALL`) at the back, a path down a green with a stone well and the village's goods, and with `spec.fort` 1+ a palisade round it all, its gate at the front. The hall flies the flag.
  - A ground point (x, g) at height z shows at (x, g − z). So the plan reads as a plan: the courtyard, the walls' walkways and merlons, the towers' round tops and cones, and the roofs. Only the faces turned toward the viewer show, short, below their tops.
  - The gate sits on the cell's middle column (`gateX` = cell/2), so a road meets it.
  - `gallery.html?view=castles` shows them.
- **Views:** most buildings come in two views, and the seed picks one (about 2 in 5 come out top-down).
  - **Angled:** a side wall shows on the right, or mirrored on the left.
  - **Top-down:** straight on and seen from above, like the castles, with the door on the cell's middle column.

  `spec.view` (`'top'` or `'angled'`) or `spec.facing` (`'front'`, `'left'` or `'right'`) pins the view. The result's `view` says which was drawn, and a top-down one gives `gateX`. Keeps, citadels, mines, caves, cranes and lumber sheds have only the top-down view. `gallery.html?view=top` (or `angled`) shows one view.
- **Facing:** a building may face either way. `spec.facing` is `'right'` (side wall on the right) or `'left'` (side wall on the left).
- **End-on:** long buildings may stand end-on, their gable to the camera and their long side running back over 1×2 tiles. Set it with `spec.deep`; this applies to longhouses, barns, warehouses and halls.
- **Seed:** without these options, the seed picks the orientation.
- **Light:** always comes from the upper left. Each surface is shaded by its role (`LIGHT` in `buildings.js`), so turned buildings stay correctly lit. A left-facing building is drawn facing right, with the light swapped to where each surface ends up, then flipped.
- **Roofs:** carry their details:
  - a thick eave lip that shades the wall below
  - ridge caps, or a bound straw ridge for thatch
  - gable boards and a finial
  - a king post and vent in timbered gables
  - moss and worn patches
  - dormers on long roofs
- **Wings:** some houses grow a lean-to annex against the side, a porch over the door, or a gabled cross-wing at the front (the main hall then stands back). Walls of timber, daub, plank or hide stand on a stone footing.
- **Footprint:** one building fills one tile; some fill 2×1 or 2×2. Draw `shadow`, then `img`, at `tileX*16 - ax`, `(tileY+1)*16 - ay`.
- **Fit, no clipping:** a building is drawn on a roomy canvas and measured. While it is wider than its tiles, it leaves things off (barrels, annex, cart, sign…), pulls in canopies, jibs and sails, then narrows. It is then cropped to exactly its footprint: `img.width` is `tiles[0]*16` and `ax` is 0, so neighbours never overlap. It may still rise into the tile behind. `clipped` is true if anything was cut off; the game's `tests/pixels.js` checks that it never is.
- **True pixels:** every pixel is opaque or empty (the outline is one solid colour), and every anchor is a whole pixel. Draw pictures at whole-pixel positions and never scale them.
- **Variety:** comes from structure. The seed picks footprint, roof form (side gable, front gable, hip, shed, cone, battlements), storeys, wall and roof materials, and add-ons. Treehouses vary in trunk, canopy (green, evergreen or autumn), house position, ladder or steps and a second platform. Market stalls vary in canopy (sloped or peaked), stripes, goods on the counter and what stands beside them.
- **Peoples:** `style` is `human`, `dwarf`, `elf` or `orc`. It changes materials, and each people also has its own types:
  - **Elves:** treehouse, elf hall, spire.
  - **Orcs:** yurt, hide hall, watchtower.
  - **Dwarves:** stone hall, forge.

## Flags
Buildings carry no flags in their pictures; the game draws its own (animated) flag sprite. A building that flies one (keep, citadel, some towers) returns `flag: [x, y]`, the foot of its flag pole in the cell's own pixels, on a ridge or tower top. Otherwise `flag` is null. The flag takes no room in the cell (it flies above the picture), so a hall given `spec.fort` always has one. The gallery draws a stand-in flag there.

## Work places and sites
`well`, `minehead`, `lumbershed`, `crane` (a treadwheel crane for quarries), `tent` and `wagon` are building types too.

**Sites** are places on the map nobody builds, and `PX.site({type, env, seed, mat, snow, glow, banner})` draws every one. `PX.SITE_TYPES` lists them under the game's own names:
- `cave`, `ruins` and `shrine`: one tile (16×16), the same result as `PX.building`. `spec.glow` colours a shrine.
- `hideout` and `travelers`: camps, the result of `PX.camp` with `camp: true`. `spec.banner` is the travellers' flag colour.

None of them is in `PX.BUILDING_TYPES`, and `PX.building` refuses them.
- **Top-down only:** the mine, cave, lumber shed and crane.
- **Natural rock:** the cave and mine are clusters of natural boulders, not masonry, shaped by `spec.env`:
  - `grass`: grey rock with grass and moss;
  - `frost`: rock under snow, with icicles over the cave mouth;
  - `desert`: banded sandstone;
  - `swamp`: dark mossy rock, vines and a puddle. `spec.mat` sets their rock (sandstone in the desert), `spec.snow` caps them in the frost, and `spec.stilts` raises any house on posts over a bog.

## Fortification
`spec.fort` (0–3) sets the walls of a keep or citadel: 0 none, 1 a palisade with wooden towers, 2 stone walls, 3 great walls (taller, a tower halfway along each side). On a hall (any building with a roof) a fortification of 1 or more puts a palisade fence round it, its gate on the middle. A hall given `spec.fort` also flies the holding's flag.

## Levels
`spec.lv` (2 or 3) draws a better version of the same building in the same cell: a richer roof, a chimney, more windows and a porch, then a second storey, dormers and lanterns. Work places gain their gear.

## Docks
A pier is drawn without water, to be laid over real water tiles.

## Loading
In a page: `<script src="../engine/pixel.js"></script>`, then `buildings.js`, then `pieces.js`, then `ground.js`, then `worldnature.js` (it reads `ground.js`'s `PX.GROUND_PAL`).
