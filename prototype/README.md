# Ashmark prototype

Open `Ashmark.html` in a browser. There is no build step and nothing to install.

## Independent army groups (save schema v2)

Start a **new campaign** for this version. Old saves are not loaded or migrated; old browser storage is left untouched.

- Each army has at most **10 groups**, including its hero/leader and companions. Wounded companions in your band reserve their slot; companions leading another army belong to that army instead.
- Each troop group contains one unit type. Duplicate types have persistent, distinct IDs. The existing total-headcount capacity remains a separate limit; no new per-group soldier cap is imposed.
- Open **Hero → Party → Manage** to name a group (up to 32 characters), split an exact number of fit soldiers, or merge compatible groups. A split starts with its default name and leaves wounded with its original group. **Reinforce** selects a recruitment target.
- Use **↑ / ↓** to reorder the entire army, including hero and companion groups. This persists into battle cards, default number keys and deployment lanes. Wounded companions reserve a slot but have no battle card until recovered. Custom Ctrl+number selections still override the default keys within a battle.
- Each group shows its own wounded count. Groups with no fit soldiers appear under **Recovering groups** and retain their name when soldiers return. They do not take a battle card or an active slot until restored.
- Battles show one card per group. Shift-click adds cards to the selection. **1–9 and 0** select the ten cards; Ctrl+number stores a custom selection. **Home** resets the battle camera (0 is now the tenth card).
- The cards carry no words (`battlehud.js` `cardStatus`): key, portrait, count, shape and formation, the health/morale/stamina bars and up to `CARD.show` (4) status icons by rank: failing morale (with how many run or break), order (none while holding), pace while moving, an ability on, out of ammunition or exhausted, flags, a character falling back, low ammunition or tired, hidden or in woods, confident, weaknesses. The words are in the card's `title`/`aria-label` (a long press on touch), each icon's in its SVG `<title>`. The All card: the troops icon, the total, and running/breaking counts.
- Every card status icon is the same round badge (`moraleicons.js` `badgeSVG`: the 9×9 `BADGE`, a dark inside, a white 5×5 `GLYPH`), as in the details and beside the banners; its rim shows its kind (`BADGE_KIND` → `BADGE_CAT`): order gold, pace pale grey, ability on green, warning amber, bad red, flags steel blue, sight teal; morale and weaknesses keep their own rims. The order bar buttons keep their 9×9 `ORDER_ICON`/`ABIL_ICON` art.
- The banners over the groups (`moraleicons.js` `iconLayout`) each stay over their own group, eased after it on the battle clock (deploying, with the clock frozen and groups placed at once, simply put over it: `tests/dragcancel.js`); they may overlap (selected, then hovered, then yours over theirs; one underneath faded). Only overlapping banners of groups at blows with each other fan out side by side, eased over `ICON_FAN.ease` (0.25 s); `reducedMotion()` places them at once. A click picks the topmost banner; a second click on the same spot cycles to the next one underneath (`iconPick`).
- Orders, formation, facing, waypoints and capability-filtered ability toggles are independent for each group. Enemies and allied armies use the same grouping model; reinforcements keep their own army budgets and source identities.
- Fought-battle casualties and wounded returns retain the originating troop-group ID. Merging groups transfers wounded ownership. Wounded whose original group cannot fit wait rather than reinforcing an unrelated duplicate.
- Fought-battle results include a per-group report in battle-card order: starting soldiers, fit survivors, new wounded and killed. Medicine returns count as fit; extra rearguard deaths after a failed escape belong to the actual group that lost them. Destroyed groups retain their name in this report. Earlier wounded are excluded. Hero/companion conditions appear separately and never count as soldier deaths. Auto-resolve and non-field encounters retain their aggregate losses summary rather than inventing a field breakdown.
- **Town → Recruit** lets you choose a destination group (or one new group), enter a soldier quantity, review total gold/headcount/slots, then confirm. Orders are all-or-nothing. Single-soldier quick-hire buttons remain available.
- Click a training-tree row to **upgrade a whole group**: select the source group, review its total gold and pooled XP cost, then confirm. Its ID, soldier count and slot are preserved, even in a full ten-group army; formation resets to Line. Groups with wounded waiting to return must heal first. Individual **Train 1** buttons remain available and may need a free slot for a new type.
- Recruitment, transfers and training reject operations that need an unavailable slot. A changed price or unavailable order is rechecked at confirmation rather than silently charging different terms.
- Open a companion’s details → **Army transfers** to select the exact source formation, quantity and destination (compatible group or a separate new group). Review and confirm the whole order; capacity, proximity, leadership and army slots are rechecked. Only fit soldiers move, leaving wounded with the source army. A whole fit formation moved to a new group keeps its name, formation and frontage; a partial new group uses defaults, and reinforcement keeps the destination’s settings. IDs are local to the receiving army.
- Taking the last soldiers back reserves a slot for the returning companion as well as any new troop group. Automatic **Come back** preserves separate formations and their settings too; if the entire returning army plus its leader cannot fit, no soldiers move. Automatic return retains the prior over-headcount-cap policy; explicit transfers cannot exceed headcount capacity. Giving to a distant companion remains allowed, but taking soldiers back requires being within 60 pixels. Neither direction may change a party currently in a battle. Garrisons still convert stationed soldiers into generic defenders.
- Individual attacks, fatigue, weapon rules and morale remain the existing systems. The proposed shared group-resolve / unanswered-missile-fire morale redesign is **not implemented** by this change. Troop XP is still pooled by unit type.

`core/army.js` owns group identity and mutations. `P.troops` and NPC `tc` remain count views/interchange records for economy and world simulation; `ArmyRoster` updates them together with `army.groups`. Battle controls reference unique IDs rather than fixed Infantry/Archers/Mages/Cavalry buckets. Tactical role is a separate field used by the captain.

Focused regression suites: `armygroups`, `armyedges`, `groups`, `battlesetup`, `battlecommands`, `rts`, plus the existing recruitment, roster, laboratory, combat and morale suites. Test-only `testGroup(role)` resolves role-based execution fixtures to real group IDs; it is not a gameplay or save-compatibility API.

`wiki.html` is the game reference: world map, biomes, races, realms, holdings, the economy, battle, troops, gear, monsters and life on the map. It loads the game's own data and sprite scripts, so it updates itself whenever the data changes.

The scripts are plain `<script>` files, not modules, so the page still works from `file://`. Their top-level `const` and `function` names are shared across files. The rules that follow (checked by `tests/structure.js`):

- **One name, one place.** Every top-level name is global; a second `function` with the same name would silently replace the first.
- **Load order.** `core/core.js` first, then all of `data/`, then the systems (story, world, battle, scene, gfx, ui). Code that runs at load time (data tables, event listeners, `onAct` registrations) goes after everything it uses. `wiki.html` loads the same list (minus `ui/input.js` and `main.js`); keep the two in step.
- **Data, not special cases.** Tunable numbers and kinds of things live in `js/data/`. A new monster is a `MONSTERS` entry; one that roams the whole map, like the dragon, adds a `mythic` block. The cost of a deed is a `CONDUCT` entry.
- **Systems and saves.** A system's daily and per-frame work is one `Systems.add({name, order:{day, frame}, day, frame, ends})` line in its own file; `world.js` `dayTick` and `updateWorld` only run `Systems.day()` and `Systems.frame(d, dt)` (a frame with `ends` returning true ends the frame). The order is everything (one shared `Math.random`): `tests/structure.js` pins it and the world fingerprints hold it. A piece of the world's save is one `Saves.add({key, order, save, load, settle, defaults})` (`save` returning undefined leaves its key out); `save.js` `worldSave` / `worldLoad` run them. Keep the saved keys in their order (W7, B2 hash the save). Design: `doc/CORE_DESIGN.md` K1.
- **Actions.** Every button names an action `prefix-name` (`data-act="cv-buy"`). A system claims its prefix once with `onAct('cv-', (a,arg)=>G.cv?asParty(G.cv).act(a,arg):true)`; a second claim throws. Actions without a registered prefix go to `act()` in `ui/input.js` (the menu, encounters); a town window's own (`tab`, `bld`, `hire`, `buy`, `contract`, `ransom` …) go on to `townAct` in `ui/town.js`, which hands each to the place whose verb it is (`Place` `verbs`: `PLACES`, `definePlace`).
- **Settings.** Each remembered setting is one entry of `PREFS` (`ui/ui.js`): its default, how its browser-storage key `ashmark.<key>` reads and writes, and what a change does (`apply`). `prefSet(k,v)` changes and stores one (storage may be blocked: the value still changes for the session); the globals other files read (`GRID.on`, `BSPEED.v`, `GUIDE.on`, `HPBARS.on`, `BLOOD.on`, `BDEPLOY.on`, `AUTOSK.on`, `BAUTO.on`) are views over it. The Settings buttons go through `settingAct`. A new setting is a `PREFS` entry (plus its row on the Settings page). Tests: `tests/uiprefs.js`.
- **Encounters.** A new kind of fight adds its entry to `ON_WIN` in `battle/engage.js`: what winning it pays and changes.

### Shared helpers and cleanup

**The core parts** (doc/CORE_DESIGN.md, K2; loaded after `data/`, before `core/state.js`; checked by `tests/coreparts.js`):
- `core/clock.js` `Clock`: the day, `hour`, `frac` (the share of today), `isNight`/`isDay`, `part()` (Dawn… Before dawn), `darkness()`, `worldTime()` (clock units), `hours()` (hours, the road's clock), `hoursSince`, `daysSince`, `every(n)`, `year()`/`dayOfYear()` (`CLOCK.year` 120 days), `passHours(h)` and `advance(u)` (updateWorld's step: `dayTick` on a new day, then `Systems.hour(h)` on each new hour). It reads `G.day`/`G.clock` in the old helpers' very arithmetic; `timeOfDay`, `darkness`, `worldTime`, `roadNow`, `roadDay`, `roadHours`, `dayPart`, `watchNow` stay as its aliases.
- `core/timed.js` `Timed.of(record, field?)`: `has`, `end`, `daysLeft`, `set(state, days)`, `extend` (only later), `clear`, `onEnd` + `check()`; a view over the saved end-day fields (a holding's `fear`, `raided`, `sacked`, `turmoil`; `P.hostile[fac]`; `t.mem`), so saves are unchanged. `until(t,k)` (siege.js) is its alias.
- `core/bus.js`: `worldOn(type, fn)`, `worldOff`, `worldEmit(type, data)`, the world's twin of `battleOn`; the world's events are named `noun:verb` and listed in its header (who emits each, its data, who hears it): your deeds (`band:beaten`, `monster:slain`, `site:cleared`, `lord:beaten`, `holding:razed`) heard by the quests; what happens in the world (`siege:laid`, `siege:broken`, `holding:taken`, `holding:raided`, `estate:burnt`, `lord:routed`, `caravan:robbed`, `caravan:beaten`, `fight:ended`, `trace:left`, `land:threat`) heard by the news; `encounter:ended`, `incident:ended`, `quest:ended` (nobody listens yet). Listeners run at once, in the order added (S3: `questNotify` and the hooks' `newsRecord` calls are gone into it). The HUD is still redrawn by hand (`updateHUD(true)`): the suites call it and read `#stats` at once.
- `core/rng.js`: `rngOf(name)`, a mulberry32 stream per name seeded by the world, the game's number (`Rng.game`: one draw of Math.random as a new game begins, `Rng.newGame`; saved) and the name (`next`, `int`, `chance`, `pick`, `weighted`; `Rng.states()`/`restore`; `Rng.use(name, fn)`: a shared helper's Math.random draws taken from the stream while fn runs). S4: the story and the content that gets tuned often draw from their own (`road`: the encounters and their choices' rolls; `letters`; `incidents`; `quests`; `critters`: the wild game and the herds; `monsters`), so adding a kind or changing a weight never moves the world's Math.random nor the fingerprints; the world's simulation and the battles stay on Math.random. Saved last (`rng`: the game's number and the streams' states), started afresh by a new game.
- `core/spatial.js` `Spatial`: `nearest(list, x, y, filter?)`, `nearestHolding(x, y, filter?)`, `within(list, x, y, r)`, and `index(list, cell)`, a grid that answers as the full scan does (ties too). `nearestOf` (realms.js) is its alias.

**The story parts** (doc/STORY_DESIGN.md, S0–S3, S5; `js/story/`: storyplace, context, outcome and choice loaded right after `ui/window.js`; kinds, happening, encounter, incident, quest, thread and gathering right before `world/incidents.js` (a Happening is a `WorldObject`, parties.js; the road events are Incident kinds); checked by `tests/story.js`, `tests/kinds.js` and `tests/threads.js`):
- `story/storyplace.js` `StoryPlace`: what a spot on the map is (terrain, climate, land, road, cover), each worked out once; `story/context.js` `storyCtx()` (`Story.ctx`): a `StoryContext` (a `StoryPlace` where you stand) for you: where (`terrain`, `climate`, `land`, `owned`, `nearHold`, `nearest`, `nearestDist`, `byRoad`, `byMountains`, `landKind`, `woodsEdge`, `cover`, `by(type, r)`; worked out once, when first read), when (`day`, `hour`, `isDay`, `isNight`, `dark`: the Clock) and who (`troops`, `size`, `cap`, `power`, `renown`, `gold`, `morale`, `food`, `skill(k)`, `honor(f)`, `rep(name)`: read live), and `weather`. Inside `Story.frame(fn)` (the road's moment, `updateRoad`) one is made and shared; outside, each call is fresh. `Story.at(tx, ty)`: a tile's where-part (the spot rules, `ENC_ON`). The road's kinds' `ok(c)` read it (`roadPick` passes one for the whole roll).
- `story/outcome.js` `Outcome`: an outcome is plain data (`{gold:20, honor:[fac,-5], morale:-3, say:'…'}`), each key done by its handler in the order written: `gold` (or `{n, min}`), `food`, `goods`, `gear`, `troops`, `lose`, `wounded`, `rep`, `honor`, `renown`, `morale` (or `{n, min, max}`), `xp`, `train`, `sp`, `hours`, `vitals`, `mood`, `wrong` (a CONDUCT entry), `log`, `news`, `fight`, `chase`, `end`, `gone`, `start` (another encounter), `band`, `call` (a hook's own step). `Outcome.apply(o, {e})` (`applyOutcome`) returns what came of it (`res.goods`, `res.gear`, `res.cost`… and `res.say`, the line); `Outcome.facts(o)` / `describeOutcome(o)` its words. No handler draws Math.random.

## State and inventory

Inventory and equipment rules live outside the screens. What each bag record is comes from its item kind (`core/items.js`, above). `Inventory` operates on a record's current bag, with capacity and stacking supplied to it. Nothing is refused up to its limit (doc/ITEMS_DESIGN.md §4a): what does not fit goes into overflow slots past its `size`, up to `BAG.maxOver` (15), each costing `BAG.per` (4%) of your pace, down to `BAG.floor` (×0.4): `burden()`, read by `bandPace` (`bagPace`); past that `BAG_FULL` ("You cannot carry any more"), a quest item excepted (`add(k, id, n, force)`). `over`, `spare`, `settle()` (the overflow moved into slots freed since), `put(rec, force)`; the card's Burdened tag (`burdenTag`, `render.js updateHUD`); the bag window shows the overflow slots in red. It carries only what its category routes to the bag; `gained` tells an item's kind that it came in. `EquipmentService` operates on a wearer's gear and an `Inventory`, returning `{ok, reason, moved}` without touching the DOM or logging. Hero and companion commands use the same service; companion slot limits remain explicit. A failed transfer restores both records, including when removing a pack or mount would shrink a full bag. Equipment transfers always return existing items to the bag; only rewards use the loot pile.

`GameSession` resets the live `G`, `P`, and `W` records in place from factories. New game and load share this reset, so missing save fields use defaults and transient input, loot and battle state cannot carry over. Record identity and the player's `Party` prototype survive; saves still contain plain JSON. `session.js` loads after `world/realms.js`, which creates `W`; its commands run after boot has loaded every system. A new game (`begin`) and a load (`loadGame`) run as steps (`runSteps`, `ui/loading.js`): the overlay shows only past `LOADING.delay` (300 ms), `G.mode` is `'loading'` meanwhile (no simulation), and `LOADING.sync` (the tests) runs them at once. ## Supplies

Travel is not free (doc/SUPPLY_DESIGN.md; `world/supply.js`, every number in `SUPPLY`). Your band's stamina (`P.bsta`, 0-100) is your
hero's: it is kept in her `P.sp`, on her scale (`maxSP`; when that grows, by a level, the share dips a little). Each hour (the system
`supply`, `Systems.hour`) the band:

  2 morale an hour, and after a day men desert or fall sick by turns;

  it), all of the category Water. Water and liquor are goods in the bag (`data/places.js` `DRINK_GOODS`) that no market trades: kept out of `GOODS`'s keys,
  so no holding stocks them. Waterskins fill free beside a river, lake or oasis (stopped) or at a holding's well; the tavern's
  tapster sells liquor (`tapsterCard`);
- **tires or rests**: −3 an hour on the march (×1.5 rough ground, ×1.3 at night, ×1.5 Forced March, ×1.5 with nothing to drink,
  ×2 in the desert with no water, up to ×1.5 short of furs in the frost; ×0.7 mostly mounted, −3% a level of Athletics or Riding,
  ×0.9 a Guide; every drink alike); +2 stopped, +8 in camp, +10 staying in a holding (half with nothing to drink; none with no
  food, nor in the desert with no water). `staminaRate(moving, drink, land)` says how much and why;

  `SUPPLY.drink`: ale +0.5, liquor +1.5 a ration; water none), not by the hour while drunk;
- under 20 is **Tired** (pace ×0.85: `bandPace`, morale −1 every 3 hours), under 5 **Exhausted** (pace ×0.6, a man falls sick every
  8 hours); the desert's thirst (after 12 hours) and frostbite (6 hours' worth of shortage) take men too, as wounded.

- **Into battle** (`battle/fatigue.js`): your troops, companions and hero start at the band's stamina (never under

  standing ended with. Not in a tournament or the lab.
- **Make camp** (Z, the dock's Camp, the action ring): stamina +8 an hour, the wounded mend ×1.5, food and drink at the rest rate;

## Formations and unit abilities

## Group morale, confidence and routing

Morale is owned by the **formation**, not the soldier. `BattleResolve` (`battle/morale.js`) holds one morale value, confidence, cohesion, recent-loss strain, missile pressure, breaking timer and route/rally/shatter state per group (`g.resolve`). Ungrouped breakable soldiers get a one-man resolve; `resolveOf(u)` finds either. Soldier fields (`u.mor`, `u.rout`, `u.confidence`, …) are **mirrors** written by `sync()`; writing them changes nothing. HP, armour, shields, ammunition and stamina stay per soldier. Heroes, companions, lords, chieftains and beasts (`steady`) have no resolve and never break.

**Inputs.** Wounds (`hurt`), deaths (`death`), flank/rear blows and charges on any member feed the group. Ratios use the group's **starting** headcount and health, so the same share of losses shakes small and large groups equally. Casualties and wounds also lower the recovery `ceiling()`. Deaths in a short window build **recent-loss strain** (up to `GROUP_RESOLVE.strainCap`), which decays with `LOSS_MEMORY.life`; bursts hurt more than spread-out losses. **Unanswered missile fire** (missile damage in the last `window` seconds, while dealing back less than `answer` of it) drains up to `missileRate` a second and blocks confidence. Per-soldier flank and charge shocks are divided by the group's starting size.

**Context.** Every 0.2 combat seconds (sooner after a death) each group samples three members (left, centre, right along its front) and averages their view: visible, distance-weighted strength within `CONFIDENCE.radius` gives **Confident** or **Outmatched**; close strength or threats on opposite sides give **Locally overwhelmed**; aligned, settled neighbours within `COHESION.radius` give **cohesion**, which softens ordinary and routing shock. Trees and rocks block the view. Confidence (1.25:1 to 3:1) adds up to +12 above the starting value and 30% shock resistance; a withdrawal order, missile pressure, a nearby rout or a fallen leader suppress it.

**Breaking and routing.** At 0 or below the group is **Breaking**: it routs after `RESOLVE.breakDelay` (0.6 s), `severeDelay` (0.25 s) at −10 or worse, or `leaderDelay` (0.2 s) during leader panic. Positive morale resets the episode. All break decisions are made before neighbour shocks are applied, so routs cannot domino in one update. On a route the group's order becomes `'rout'`, its target and queue are cleared, and every `BattleControl` order method returns early; each soldier still escapes on its own (`routStep`). A group left quiet for `rallyQuiet` seconds can rally once (back to Hold); a second rout shatters it. A nearby rout shocks other groups within `routeRadius` at most once per second. Leader death shocks every group of that side and suppresses confidence. Nearby enemy deaths reassure by `killGain`, capped at `killCap` per rolling second and at the ceiling.

**Readers.** The HUD (`grpMorale`, unit cards, badges), the battle lab's morale history and the developer panel all read the resolve. The developer panel only allows editing `group.<id>.resolve.mor`. The enemy commander reads it too: once its strength is under `FOE_CMD.beaten` (0.4) of yours and its formations' shared morale, weighted by men, is under `FOE_CMD.broken` (30), the whole enemy army **falls back** (withdraws off its own edge, logged "The enemy falls back.") instead of fighting to the last man. Once begun it does not turn round; withdrawing groups are left alone by `captainFinish`. Escaped enemies are not kills, as with a rout. Your army (even under AI command), allies, beasts and morale-free battles never fall back on their own (`beatenArmy` in `battle/commander.js`, tests in `tests/groupmorale.js`).

**Unit-level AI morale.** Every captain (the enemy, your allies, your army under AI command) also reads each group's own resolve, tuned by `UNIT_AI` in `battle/commander.js`. (1) An infantry group under `pullAt` (25), with another infantry group of the same captain still steady (`backAt`, 45, not routed, not recovering), **pulls back** `pullDist` (90 px) straight away from the foe's middle and stands there (`g.recover`) until its morale is back to `backAt` or `maxRecover` (15 s) pass; a time-capped group cannot pull back again for another `maxRecover` s. A group with no steady sibling fights on. (2) The nearest steady sibling not already fighting **plugs the gap**: it is ordered once onto the foe nearest the recovering group (`g.plug`) and keeps at him while the other recovers. (3) Cavalry under `chargeAt` (40) **start no new charge** (from the flank, the regroup, a rescue or the ranged-duel ride): they stand off as after a charge until they recover, or at most `cavRecover` (4) s (losses lower the ceiling morale recovers to, so worn riders would otherwise stand off for ever); a charge under way goes on. (4) Cavalry target choice adds `prey` (2) and shooters' focus fire `preyShot` (1.5) for a foe whose group is **wavering**. Groups without a resolve (heroes, companions, leaders, beasts) count as steady and are never weak prey; with morale off (`B.noMorale`) none of this applies. Tests: `tests/unitmorale.js`.

**The word of a lost group** (the owner, 2026-10-20; `MORALE.army`, `armyWord`, `armyHear`). When a group routs or is cut down to the last man, every other group of its side feels it, each later the farther it stands, as word spreads: after `delay` (0.5 s) + `per` (0.5 s) a 100 px, at most `max` (4.5 s; `B.armyWord` holds the word on its way). The hit: `hit` (6) × the lost group's starting size against its side's average group (clamped 0.4-2) × `elite` (1.4, its men tier 3 or more on average) × `lead` (1.5, a hero's or leader's own group) × nearness (1 beside it, down to `far` 0.45 at `span` 900 px) × `fade`^n (0.7; n: the word that group has heard already, `r.armyHeard`, so losses stack, each a little less) × the hearer's steadiness, as an ordinary shock (confidence, cohesion and Stand Fast! soften it). **No double count:** a rout's neighbours within the cascade's `GROUP_RESOLVE.routeRadius` (140 px) take the cascade (`MORALE.cascade`, at once, with its panic) and never the word; a group cut down has no cascade, so its neighbours hear the word too; a group is lost once (`r.armyLost`: routing, then cut down, is one word). A routing group hears nothing. A hit of `say` (3) or more puts the word over the group that hears it (`moraleSay`, the same word said once there): "The left flank is broken!", "The right flank has fallen!", "The centre is broken!", by where the lost group stood in its army facing the enemy. `MORALE.army.on` false: only the cascade, as before. Tests: `tests/armymorale.js`.

Tuning lives in `MORALE` (with `MORALE.army`), `CONFIDENCE`, `AWARENESS`, `COHESION`, `LOSS_MEMORY`, `RESOLVE` and `GROUP_RESOLVE`. Coverage: `tests/groupmorale.js` (shared state, normalisation, routing as a group, rally/shatter, HUD/lab agreement), `tests/moralebalance.js` (kill reassurance, breaking timers), `tests/confidence.js`, `tests/moralecontext.js`, `tests/moraleresilience.js`, `tests/armymorale.js` (the word of a lost group), and the reports `tests/visual/moraleaudit.js` and `tests/visual/confidenceaudit.js`; footmen against berserkers in `tests/moralebalance.js`.

## AI tactics

**Shooters lead, or play safe.** Every captain picks a posture for its shooters each think (`rangedMode`, stored in the captain's memory `captainMem(ck).rmode`): **aggressive** when attacking, not outnumbered, out-ranging (×1.1) or out-shooting (×1.25) the other side, with little fast melee against them (riders or speed ≥ 60 under 15% of its shooters' strength), quivers and nerve whole and no melee foe closing. The shooters then lead: the army line puts the infantry `screenGap` behind their full reach, and they go up (at a run from over 80 px) and open fire first. When the captain turns **safe** (defending, outnumbered, cavalry, worn, a foe closing), shooters that led run back behind their infantry. The margins relax while aggressive, and a 2 s wait applies before leading again.

**Charges by stamina** (`runFor`): fresh infantry runs from 240 px and charges from a 140 px gap (not at riders or beasts, nor with a charge coming at it, when it braces instead); fresh riders gallop all the way, start a charge from 160 px, and pull back 150 px to charge again; less fresh, `CAPT.runAt`; tired, 130 px; spent, a walk. Tired riders stand off after a charge until stamina 55, or 5 s.

**Characters fall back** (`CHAR_FALL`): one who rolls out of a press ("Falling back") goes to cover behind its own troops, heals if it can, and goes back in once its breath is back and the place is no trap (at most 2 foes near the target, or as many of ours there): your hero to your order, AI characters to the safest foe near; after 12 s, or with no troops of its own left, it goes in anyway. Any order of yours to its group (a move, an attack, Hold, pace (Charge/Advance or R), a formation or a flag, a Shift waypoint) ends it — a unit ability does not (`playerOrder` in commands.js; tests `tests/orderfallback.js`) — and with Auto skills off your characters never fall back on their own. Back in at its own pick, a character isn't drawn into a duel for 2 s (`CHAR_FALL.keep`). Health is not a gate (it doesn't regenerate in battle).

**Auto (A):** A hands the selected groups to the AI (`g.aiCmd`, this battle only), or with nothing selected your whole army (`B.auto`, remembered: `BAUTO`); `deploy.js` `aiHas`/`aiAll`/`aiAny`. Your captain orders only the groups it has (`captainGroups`), counts the rest as friends (`assess`) and never orders them; the commander powers are its only with the whole army. Handing groups over starts its captain afresh (`aiReset` clears every AI note); any order, formation, flag or ability button of yours takes back the groups it is given to (`takeCommand(gs)`); groups you sent withdrawing keep withdrawing. Posture keeps attacking down to `attackAt − FOE_CMD.hold` and is remembered per captain (your army and your allies apart). Cavalry pick a flank once and keep it; riders 3 s in melee on any charge, a rescue included, pull back to charge again; hunting archers head home for at least 3 s without focus-firing at a run. Only a wavering group not engaged hand to hand pulls back (an engaged one fights on), and a braced group is never sent to plug a gap. Tests: `tests/controlaudit.js`, `tests/aishare.js`.

**Attack-move (Ctrl + right-click, or D / the button then a ground order):** `BattleControl.attackMove` marches to the place; `amStep` turns the group on the nearest foe its side sees within `AMOVE` reach of its march (melee 90 px from any man, riders 120, shooters their shortest reach + 30): fighters attack his group, riders charge, shooters halt and shoot at will (just short: on into reach); none left (off × the reach, no flicker) and it marches on to the same place, its waypoints kept, then holds; runners are chased `AMOVE.leash` px from where it turned at most. Shift queues one; any new order of yours (`amEnd`) ends it, the pace does not. No field on a group never given one (the fingerprints stay as they were). Tests: `tests/attackmove.js`.

**Side colours:** one table, `battle.js` `SIDE` (yours blue, your allies' green, the enemy's red; a paler shade routing), read by the soldiers' cloth (`spriteTeam`), health bars, icons, the small map, order paths, words and callouts.

**Race fighting styles** (`TEMPERAMENT` in `battle/commander.js`): each captain (the enemy, your allies, your army under Auto) takes a temperament from its troops (the race of 60% of those that have one, else *mixed*; beasts, monsters and generic levies get none), chosen once at its first think (`captainMem(ck).temper`): multipliers on existing tunables, read through `tun()` at the captain's decision points. **Human** is the base itself. **Dwarves hold the line** (attack only from a higher strength ratio, hold longer, run in from nearer, shield wall against riders earlier, rotate a wavering group out sooner). **Elves skirmish** (shooters lead on smaller margins, a wider screen behind them, infantry goes in after longer volleys, riders swing wider round a flank). **Orcs come on in a frenzy** (attack sooner, charge and run from further, pull back only when nearly broken, fight on long after a human army would fall back, riders rest less). **A mixed host** favours wavering prey. The enemy's style is logged as the battle opens. Balance: in human-troop mirror labs over seeds 1–8 each style wins 38–50% against human. Tests: `tests/aitemperament.js`.

**The captains read the ground** (`TERRAIN_AI` and `TERRAIN_TEMPER` in `battle/commander.js`; `tests/aiterrain.js`). With no terrain the captains are unchanged.
- **Defence:**
  - A defending army takes the crest of a top near its line on its own half, if it can get there before the enemy's fighters could run there.
  - Its infantry stands on the rim and its shooters behind it on the height; elves may hold a wood.
  - A river between the armies makes a defence more likely, and defenders hold their bank at the crossings.
- **Attack:**
  - Infantry does not run uphill into a held crest: it walks up after longer volleys and runs only the last 40 px.
  - Riders wait rather than charge foot holding a hilltop, ride round woods to an open flank spot, and pass over marks standing in woods, marsh or water.
- **Shooters** stand on the highest ground near their place, out of woods (elves stand under the trees).
- **Rivers:** moves over a river go by the nearest ford or bridge, with files narrowed to it.
- **Races:** dwarves look further for high ground and hold crossings longer; orcs charge up anything short of a two-level climb.

**Beasts in battle** (`BEAST_TACTICS` in `battle/beastmind.js`; `tests/beastai.js`). Under an AI captain (the enemy's, your allies', yours under Auto) each group of beasts fights its kind's way, taken out of the captain's arms first, so in a mixed horde wolves circle while trolls hit the front. Beasts never rout; your beasts follow your orders.
- **The look:** no ranks: every group of beasts stands in the ragged `pack` spread (`FORMS.pack`, not one of your formations). At the start an AI group of wolves splits into packs of 3–5 (`id~2`, `id~3`…), boars into twos and threes, bears and trolls into loners or pairs.
- **Wolves (`pack`):** the packs pick one prey together (routing, shooters, wounded, alone; distance counts most) and circle it, each to a side of its own from its rear round, the shorter way and only where a way can be walked (`canReach`: no stall at a cliff). All on their sides (or 3 s), they bite at once; after 4 s at grips a pack pulls back 60 px and comes round to a fresh side, unless the prey's morale is under 50 (then it worries on). A prey bitten from two sides at once is **surrounded**: 6 morale a think. A pack that loses 75% of its health in one bite breaks off 140 px and gathers; too few left, it joins another pack.
- **Boars (`rush`):** headlong at the nearest, 2.5 s at grips, 70 px back (a run-up: the next charge lands with full momentum), again.
- **Bears, trolls (`brute`):** straight at the thickest fight (most of a group's men at grips with theirs), else the strongest group; the dragon at the most crowded one (its breath as ever).
- **Spiders, scorpions, the tiger, the wyrm (`ambush`):** into the nearest spot deep in a wood (the wyrm: where it is), unseen (`seenBy`); a seen foe within 130 px (tiger, scorpion 110; wyrm 150), a wound while waiting, or 15 s (tiger 20, wyrm 8), and they strike; sprung, they are seen by those they fight.
- **Griffins (`swoop`):** mark the shooters (else the foe's edges and rear), circle to the mark's rear, dive, wheel 160 px away, come again from the other side. **Slimes (`blob`):** a slow advance.
- **Close in:** within 70 px of its mark a group charges, each beast at the foe nearest him (one set on a single man of a crowd would stand idle behind the others); one that spits (spiders, slimes) stands and spits once its mark is in reach (on a charge it would back off a foe closing on it, into a corner).
- **Off:** `BEAST_TACTICS.on=false` (the test harness, like `TERRAIN.on`), beasts fight as before: one group, a line, the captain's arms. The fingerprint suites run with it off and stay byte-identical.

Balance (`ai:true` lab, terrain and fog on, 500 px apart, seeds 1–8; men's wins of 8, length, men lost, before → after): wolves ×10 vs 10 foot + 6 bows 4, 19 s, 52% → 7, 29 s, 46%; boars ×4 vs 8 + 4: 8, 19 s, 25% → 8, 18 s, 15%; trolls ×2 vs 8 + 4: 8, 37 s, 28% → 8, 39 s, 33%; spiders ×12 vs 6 + 4 in a forest: 6, 50 s, 19% → 7, 36 s, 21% (every strike from hiding); griffins ×2 vs 8 + 10 on hills: 2, 26 s, 78% → 2, 42 s, 77%; a mixed horde (8 wolves, a troll, 3 boars, 4 spiders) vs 10 + 6 + 2 knights: 7, 45 s, 34% → 8, 37 s, 37%. A wolf battle sees about 4–5 bites from 3 packs, 3 regroups and pull-backs, and 2–3 surrounded thinks; boars rush twice, griffins dive twice.

Tunables: `AI_TACTICS` in `battle/commander.js`, `CHAR_FALL` in `battle/charmind.js`, `BEAST_TACTICS` in `battle/beastmind.js`. Tests: `tests/aitactics.js`, `tests/charfallback.js`, `tests/beastai.js`. Balance: with the AI rallying, equal infantry fights rout at ~46–49% losses (~8 s).

## Charges, bracing and morale-off rules (effect audit, 2026-10-17)

- **Spearmen brace** only against a charge on their **front**, and only while fighting (not routing or withdrawing): no charge bonus, push or shock. Brace (the ability) extends that to the flanks; a charge into the **rear** always hits home (`spearBraced` in morale.js).
- **Depth and wedge scale only the charge bonus**: a block 4+ ranks deep takes 70% of the bonus (not from behind), so a weak charge never does less than no charge; a full 16-rider wedge is about ×2.17.
- **Charge stamina** is spent only when the charge actually earns its bonus (cavalry from `CHARGE.min`, foot from `CHARGE.foot_min`; characters likewise).
- **Morale off** (some lab/test battles) skips only morale effects: flank/rear damage and foot-charge damage still apply. Tournaments still have no flank or foot-charge bonus.
- Morale boosts above the recovery ceiling (Rally, war cry, an enemy leader's fall) fade at `GROUP_RESOLVE.fade` per combat second. Tests: `tests/effectaudit.js`.

## Battle physics

`battle/physics.js` (`PHYSICS`, one tunables table; `PHYSICS.on`, off in `tests/harness.js` so every older fingerprint is unchanged, on in `tests/physics.js`). Every soldier has a **mass** by kind and armour (a man by his armour class, ×1.1 heavy; a rider adds his horse, 3.2; beasts by kind, a wolf 0.9 to the dragon 20), a **heading** and a **speed**. He speeds up at his kind's acceleration (less tired, on bad ground or uphill: `groundMul`), slows at its braking and turns at its rate, slower the faster he goes: foot get going in a quarter second, heavy foot in twice that, riders need a ~40 px run-up, overshoot by ~30 px and wheel round wide; a line ordered to face a flank turns in time, and the side a blow lands on follows the heading (`facing`).

- **Charges:** the damage bonus is still `CHARGE`'s, by momentum, now the run that built it capped by the speed he still has at the blow (no double count). The old push and jolt (`chargePush`, `chargeJolt`) give way to an impact by the two masses: the one struck is thrown back (`u.kx`, the slide) and, hard enough for his weight, **knocked down** for 0.5–1.4 s (no blows; then he gets up), which shakes his unit a little; ranks close behind him and a shield wall add weight; a wedge rider hits with the wedge's weight; the attacker keeps only the speed the two go on with.
- **Braced spears** (`spearBraced`: spearmen facing it, Brace for the flanks) stop a charge dead, throw the rider back, stagger him and hurt him ("Braced!").
- **Pushing:** the jostle shares the overlap by mass (the heavy shove the light, a troll walks through a line), and one pressing into a foe is slowed by him; a heavy beast's blow throws men (and knocks them down). Routers wander and stumble (the battle's own seeded stream, `physRng`).
- **Safety:** rock, cliffs, water and the edges hold as before (`terrainHold` after every move); one who runs into a wall loses his speed (`physPost`). Feel: riders throw dust braking or wheeling hard, men lean into a run, the knocked-down lie and get up, a big impact jolts the view (never with reduced motion).
- Balance (`tests/visual/physicsbalance.js`, AI on AI in the open, 8 seeds, off → on): cavalry vs light foot 2/8 → 4/8 wins; cavalry into braced spears still loses every time and harder (riders lost 80% → 100%, spears 42% → 24%); heavy vs light infantry, archers vs cavalry, a troll vs a line, wolves vs foot, knights vs militia keep their winners.

## Battle speed (spatial grid)

`battle/spatial.js` (`SpatialGrid`, open as `SG` only during the soldiers' turns, the separation pass `jostle()` and arrow flight) answers neighbour queries from per-team buckets of `B.units` indices: `nearest` searches rings outward from the soldier's cell; box queries serve `shooterTarget`, `roomyFoe`, `withdrawThreat`, charges, projectile hits and splash; `moraleView` has its own grid per morale tick. **Exactness rule:** every query returns exactly what the old full scan did (same set, same `B.units` order, same ties and float arithmetic), so battles are byte-identical; outside those phases queries fall back to the full scan. It depends on one invariant: during the soldiers' turns only the acting soldier changes position (moves through `knock`/velocity are safe) and nobody revives mid-turn. `targetMembersOf` caches a target formation's members per turn (`targetTest` must match `battlecontrol.js` `targetMembers`). Timings (`tests/visual/battleperf.js`, ms per frame): 200 soldiers 3.9 → 1.3, 400 9.3 → 2.4, 800 38.8 → 8.2, 1200 79 → 12.9.

## Terrain

**Terrain** (`battle/terrain.js`, `doc/TERRAIN_DESIGN.md`; phase 1: hills, woods, marsh; phase 2: rivers, fords, bridges; phase 3: depth-sorted trees and cliff faces, concealment; phase 4: impassable water, cliffs, one-way hills, mountains, routing). A field fought on the world map gets a static 16-px grid `B.terrain` (kind, level 0–2, slope direction; a `BattleTerrain` since class wave E1: the ground's rules are its methods, `speedFor`, `climbOf`, `blocksView`…, its routing `B.terrain.routes` (`TerrainRoutes`) and the sight `B.sight` (`BattleSight`); the old names, `groundMul`, `seenBy`, `sightStep`…, are aliases; doc/TERRAIN_DESIGN.md §8, "Classes as built") laid out from the world tile and its 8 neighbours with its own seeded stream (the same place always gives the same field), fair to both sides (woods ≤25% of each deployment zone, the halves score within 15%, a 96-px open lane edge to edge, hills centred or mirrored); none in tournaments, sieges or raids. Layouts vary per biome (wooded hills and ridges, copses…). A field with terrain has **no scattered trees or rocks**; deserts get no woods and water only as an **oasis** beside a water tile; their sand stands **cacti, prickly pear, dry and dead shrubs, small rocks and now and then bones** in sparse clusters (greener round an oasis), decoration only: nothing collides (`engine/ground.js` `PX.desertSprite`).
- **Field variety** (2026-10-18; `doc/TERRAIN_DESIGN.md` §8, "Field variety as built"; `tests/fieldvariety.js`). The field reflects the world map.
  - **Several layouts per tile kind**, picked by the tile's seed:
    - plains: open ground, copses, a low rise;
    - hills: rolling terraces, ridges, more cliffs and ramps;
    - **peaks** (a hill among peak tiles): a pass between mountains, a saddle, a gorge;
    - swamp: marsh, a fen with dry islands and a causeway, marsh pools;
    - desert: dunes, an erg, rock spires;
    - frost: snowy copses and a frozen river even away from water (`TERRAIN_RIVER.frostP`).
  - **A forest tile is a real forest:** dense woods over 50–65% of the field (`TERRAIN_DENSE_LAYOUT`: deepwood, stands, hillwood, glade; elvenwood near an elven town; the mire wood in the swamp).
    - Each army gets a deployment glade.
    - A road crosses (a road tile), else a deer path or a chain of clearings.
    - The fairness rule replaces the 25% zone rule: both zones' cover and open ground within 8 points, the glades open, an open way from glade to glade, and the zones reaching each other.
  - **The neighbours shape the edges** (`terrainEdges`): north and south their own edge (woods, a ridge, crags, reed marsh); west and east both ends alike, so that neither army is favoured.
  - **A road tile's road** crosses the field and is a little faster (×1.1, `TERRAIN_ROAD`; the HUD shows "On the road"). Roads and paths are laid like real ones (`wayPlan`, `TERRAIN_WAY`): the least-cost line over the cells (`route.js`: woods and marsh dearer, a plateau and above all a slope band dear, rock and deep water closed, cliffs closed both ways, so a way climbs only by a slope or a ramp and crosses a river at its bridge or ford), simplified, smoothed (Chaikin) and swayed by seeded noise of varying wavelength and strength (no sine) inside its corridor; the same tile, the same road. The lab's own grounds take `road` (`true`, `'ns'` or a height share).
  - **Roads and paths are painted** (`engine/ground.js` `paintWays`, from `recipe.ways`; `WAYPAL` by climate): a road is a worn cart track 20 px wide, low in contrast, with two faint wheel ruts that wander and fade, a patchy grassy crown and a soft, ragged verge (packed snow in the frost, sand in the desert, mud in the swamp; puddles, or ice, in the ruts now and then); a deer path is a trodden strip 8 px wide, a little lighter than the ground. A hill's climbable edge is a smooth rise (its band 1 cell in from the rim: `TERRAIN.slope`), grassy, earthy or stony by stretches; only a real cliff is a grey rock face. A road runs up a rise or a ramp; trees keep their crowns off its line (a gap in the canopy zoomed out). A cliff-ringed hill's ramp is purely natural: the cliff breaks and the hillside slopes down through the gap. Where marsh lies round it, a road runs on a causeway (raised earth, logs along its sides) and a path on a boardwalk (planks across, a beam each side). Details, lone trees and reeds keep off them. An ambush's road is painted too (art only: it is no faster).
  - **Any field size:** `buildTerrain({W, H, top, bot})`. Feature counts and sizes scale with the playable area (`TERRAIN_SIZE`); dense woods lay by share. On a sized field (`BFIELD`) the ground keeps to its real deployment zones (`terrainZone`, from `fieldZone`): each army's glade lies in its zone, the fairness checks measure those zones, rock keeps out of them and the ground behind them, and a dense field's clearings spread over the half outside its zone. A mountain pass's crags grow with the field's height (`TERRAIN_ROCK.pass`) and go on along both edges.
  - **On a dense field, captains** may hold a glade (`TERRAIN_AI.gladeHold`), and riders keep to the ways (`wayRide`), on their rides and, for a mark over `TERRAIN_AI.wayCharge` (160) px off, their charges too.
  - **Big woods zoomed out** (`render.js` `LOD`): on a field of more than 2,000 trees, under zoom ×0.9, the trees are drawn as one canopy layer (`PX.battleCanopy`, painted once and kept) under the men instead of thousands of sorted sprites; zoomed in, the sprites as before (depth order, the fade over your men). The two cross-fade over 0.2 s. A huge forest's prop drawing at the whole field goes from 16–18 ms a frame to under 1 ms. Art only: sight and concealment are untouched.
  - **Lab ground keys:** `layout`, `edges {n, s, w, e}`, `road` (`true` or `'ns'`) and `elven`; `'auto'` on a forest is a forest tile.
  - **Reports:** `tests/visual/fieldbalance.js`, `tests/visual/fieldperf.js`.
- **Range:** a shooter reaches 7.5% farther per level he stands on (replaces the old whole-field +15% hill rule).
- **Charges:** the charge bonus ×1.35 downhill, ×0.65 uphill; halved against a foe in woods. **Melee:** +10% against a foe at least one level lower.
- **Climbing:** on a terrace's slope band (1 cell in from its rim), foot ×0.5, riders and big units ×0.4 (dwarves climb freely), descending ×1.15; running uphill doubles stamina drain; climbing breaks cohesion. Climbers lean forward and step short (`climbState(u)`, drawn only).
- **Woods:** foot ×0.8, riders and big units ×0.55 (elves ×1); charge momentum builds ×0.35; each wood cell stops a low missile 8%, an arrow coming down into woods 35% (elves and magic pass); three wood cells block morale sight unless the viewer stands higher; no cohesion, shield wall or wedge in woods.
- **Marsh:** foot and big ×0.7, riders ×0.5; momentum ×0.3; stamina ×1.5; blows ×0.9 (orcs and swamp beasts exempt).
- **Rivers (phase 2):**
  - **Where:** a field beside water on the world map gets a river down the middle: 48 px of deep water, meandering.
    - 1–2 fords (64 px), or a bridge (32 px) and a ford when a road bridge is within 2 tiles.
    - Frozen in the frost (all ford, crossable anywhere). A ford-only stream in half of swamps away from water.
    - None in deserts (oasis only), against monsters or in caves.
  - **Fair:** deployment zones stay dry; crossings lie near the middle, ≥120 px apart (less on a short field), and the open lane runs over one.
  - **Deep water** is impassable (phase 4): only fords and bridges cross it. `TERRAIN.wade` brings back phase 2's wading: ×0.35 speed (big ×0.5), no momentum, stamina ×2 running and spent even walking, no shooting, blows dealt ×0.75 and taken ×1.25, no cohesion, shield wall or wedge.
  - **Fords:** foot and big ×0.7, riders ×0.5, momentum ×0.3, stamina ×1.5, blows ×0.9 (orcs exempt), no shield wall or wedge.
  - **Bridges:** open ground, but narrow.
  - **Crossing:** groups stream over a bridge or ford as a column and re-form beyond it. A line holding a bank does not step into the water after a foe (`holdsBank`).
  - **For the AI:** captains read `B.terrain.ai.crossings` (`{kind, x, y, w, y0, y1, x0, x1}`) and `B.terrain.ai.river`.
- **Art:** `engine/ground.js` `PX.battleGround` paints the field once per battle (`B.ground`): terraces as cliff-faced plateaus, species-mixed woods, marsh, oases, rivers with banks, fords, bridges and ice, per-biome painted detail; the minimap shows it (`B.groundMini`).
  - `PX.battleWater` animates the water (glints and current, still with reduced motion).
  - Waders are sunk to the waist inside a ring of ripples; fords splash.
  - **Depth:** trees (32-px sprites, `PX.battleTree`) and the plateaus' south cliff faces stand in `B.ground.props` and are drawn in the same depth order as the soldiers (`render.js` `battleEnts`).
    - A man behind a trunk's foot is under its crown; one behind a rim is under the rock.
    - A tree or face covering one of your or your allies' men fades (`PROPFADE`); the enemy's get no fade.
- **Sight (phase 3):**
  - **Woods:** a soldier in a wood is unseen by a side with no soldier within 64 px of him (96 px from higher ground), unless he struck or shot at that side in the last 3 s.
  - **Raised ground** at least a level above both viewer and target hides the target too (`riseBlocked`). A `TERRAIN_GROUND` kind with `blocks:true` hides at any height, ready for mountains.
  - Sight is reckoned every 0.25 s (`sightStep`, `SIGHT`).
  - **For you:** unseen enemies are not drawn, not on the minimap, have no icon and cannot be clicked. Free fire skips them.
  - **For the AI, the same rule:** captains (`assess`) and characters (`liveFoes`) only count foes their side sees, and aim at a hidden target's last-known place (`sightGoal`).
  - **Lost targets:** an attack on a group gone from sight marches to where it was last seen, and lapses there if nothing is found.
  - **Ambush:** a group unseen until its charge lands shakes its foe ×1.5, once ("Ambush!", `AMBUSH`).
  - API: `seenBy(team,u)`, `visibleFoes(team)`, `lastSeen(team,u)`, `sightGoal(g,team)`.
- **Fog of war** (`battle/battlefog.js`, `FOG`; `B.fog`; `tests/battlefog.js`). Total War's line of sight, in every field battle (all grounds, rivers, a held bridge, an ambush; terrain on or off); none in tournaments, sieges or raids.
  - **The rule:** a side sees an enemy soldier only when one of its own (living, not routing) is within his sight range with a clear line. Ranges: foot 220 px, shooters 260, riders 300, characters 280 (the most that fits him). Big units are seen ×1.5 farther. +25% range per level the viewer stands higher. Rises and rock block the line as above; woods keep their 64/96 px rule; a blow or shot reveals the striker for 3 s.
  - **How:** `sightStep` builds each side's grid of 16-px cells (`fogGrid`): one disc per viewer cell and range, marked whole where nothing in its box stands a level above the viewer, else cast by rays (`fogCast`, the ray templates laid once a radius). Casts are kept per cell and range for the battle (the ground never changes). Cost at 800 soldiers marching: under 0.1 ms/frame (open ground and crags alike); a cold first step on crags about 10 ms.
  - **What you see:** ground outside your side's sight under a soft dark (`fogDraw`: a cell a pixel, blurred and smoothed up twice, each cell easing over 0.3 s), the same on the small map; unseen enemies undrawn, without icons, not clickable. A grey **ghost** of an enemy group (its icon, a dashed ring) marks where it was last seen; it fades over 15 s and goes when the group is seen again or its place is seen empty. All from the deployment on; the top bar keeps both armies' counts.
  - **The AI is bound the same way** (its foes are `seenBy`'s). A captain that has never seen the foe advances its army as a line after 3 s (`captainSearch`, `FOG.scout`, `FOG.probe`); its lull and posture clocks start when it first sees them.
  - **Switches:** `FOG.on` is a view over the Settings' **Fog of war (battle)** (`PREFS.bfog`, on by default; `tests/harness.js` turns it off for the older suites). The lab's `fog` key (true/false) overrides it; left out, the lab follows `FOG.on`. Scout ahead (a scout unit's ability) pushes it back round that group for a while: see Unit abilities.
- **Cliffs and rock (phase 4):**
  - **Sheer hills:** cliffs all round, one or two ramps.
  - **One-way hills:** one ramp; men may drop down the cliffs but climb only by the ramp.
  - Both are commonest on hill fields and in the frost.
  - **Mountains and rock spires** (`TKIND.rock`) are impassable and hide what lies behind them. Deserts get spires only.
  - **Fair:** each zone reaches the other and every hilltop; no rock in a zone or the open lane; ramps face both sides alike or are mirrored.
  - **Data:** `B.terrain.wall` (edge bits E 1, S 2, W 4, N 8; one-way on the low cell only) and `B.terrain.rockH` (levels of `PX.ROCK_PX` = 14 px).
- **The way round (phase 4):**
  - Every group move, chase, charge, rout and withdrawal routes round rock, cliffs and water (`route.js`, grids in `B.terrain.route`).
  - No step ever enters rock or deep water or climbs a cliff (`terrainHold`, one guard pass after all movement).
  - A charge builds momentum only on a clear last leg.
  - Captains measure tops by the way round (a ramp on the far side is far), and go looking when they have seen no one for 10 s (`captainSearch`).
  - Cost: +0.6 ms/frame at 800 soldiers on a cliff, mountain and river field; none on fields where nothing blocks.
  - Tests: `tests/routing.js`, `tests/route.js`. Reports: `tests/visual/terrainperf.js`, `tests/visual/terrainbalance.js`.
- **The world's ground (World Phase 2, behind `WORLD.gen`; doc/WORLD_DESIGN.md §13, §15.2.1):** a generated world fills its `BattleSite`s (`WorldMap#battleSite`: `river`, `crossing`, `elev`, `terrace`, `rough`, `high`); today's map leaves them null, so its fields are as before.
  - **River width:** the world's width 1, 2, 3 gives 3, 5 or 8 cells of deep water (`TERRAIN_RIVER.widths`, half 24 / 40 / 64 px; `BattleRiver.half`). A held bridge keeps it (`spec.wr`, laid over the river spec).
  - **Crossings** (the nearest within `WORLD_SCALE.battle.bridge` tiles): the world's bridge is the field's one bridge (and a ford), stone or wood as on the map; a ford's river has fords only; a ferry's one ford at its landing; none near, fords as before. *Hold the bridge* (`bridgeNear`) reads the world's bridges.
  - **Wet ground:** a delta or floodplain lays marsh beside the river in mirrored pairs, a delta's channels too (`TERRAIN_WET`, `FieldPlan#planWet`), never in a zone or the open lane.
  - **High ground** (D41, the tile's `high`): a rise is never cliffed, a cliffed hill always, a steep hill steep (`planFeatures`, `planSteep`); none: the recipe's chances.
- **Steep hills** (D41; `STEEP` in `battle/terrain.js`, `B.terrain.steep`; `tests/steephills.js`):
  - **The bank** is 2 cells a level (`STEEP.band`), climbed anywhere but slowly: foot ×0.32, riders ×0.3 (the floor: they crawl); dwarves as up a rise. Running up it tires ×3.5, walking up it tires too.
  - **Ranks break** on it (no cohesion, no shield wall or wedge) and for 2 s after the climb (`steepRanks`, morale.js).
  - **Momentum:** none builds or is kept going up; a charge down off it hits ×1.7 (a rise ×1.35). **Height:** range +15% a level (a rise +7.5%), blows down +25%.
  - **Scree:** riders keep no momentum on it; shooters on a scree hill reach 5% a level farther.
  - **A mixed hill's shoulder** is a rise; it faces up or down the field on a lone hill and is mirrored on a pair, so both armies reach it alike.
  - **Routing** prices the banks (`STEEP.route`: foot ×3, riders ×4.5 the open cost; `route.js` `routeGridOf`, the grids' `wcap`), so groups go up by a shoulder or a ramp when one is near. A steep field routes as a cliff field does.
  - **The AI** holds a steep crest before a rise (`TERRAIN_AI.steepHold`) and never runs up at one, whatever the odds.
  - **Look:** a darker stony bank, broken ledges and outcrops; scree loose grey stone; the shoulder a rise (`engine/ground.js` `slopePx`). Awaiting the owner's approval.
  - **Lab:** a hill's `steep`, `scree` and `shoulder` (degrees); `site: {river: {w, delta, flood}, crossing: {kind, stone}, high: {kind, scree, mixed, shoulder}}` (`labSite`) as a generated world's tile.
- **Battle types** (`BTYPE` in `battle/setup.js`, `B.btype`; triggers in `battle/engage.js`; only with terrain):
  - **Hold the bridge:** a fight with a lord, band, patrol or caravan on a road bridge or next to one (not in the frost, where the river is ice, nor the desert).
    - The field: a river of impassable deep water with a single bridge in the open lane near the middle; 40% of places (by the tile) also have a ford far off it, when the field is tall enough (≥100 px between).
    - The side set upon holds it: its foot on its bank by the bridge, its shooters behind, its riders further back on the flanks. You hold it unless you rode at them; holding it, your deployment zone runs to your bank (`B.dzone`).
    - The other side comes on from its own edge and must cross.
    - **AI holding:** defends while the river lies between (no lull or idle shooters send it over, until 60 s pass with no blow struck). Its foot stands at the bridge, the bridge first, 30 px back from the water until their fighters come within 260 px. It never follows anyone over the water. Its riders ride only at those who come over (`typeHolds`).
    - **AI taking:** picks the crossing least held. Its foot and riders hold back out of reach while its shooters shoot (2 volleys), then gather at the near mouth, then go over and form up 50 px beyond the far mouth. Only once the foot is formed (or 20 s pass) does the army go on as ever; its riders cross after the foot (`typeTake`).
    - The panel and the battle's start say **Hold the bridge** or **Take the bridge**.
  - **Ambush:** bands (bandits ×1, raiders and bog raiders ×1.2) or a hostile lord's army (×0.5) that come at you where there is cover spring one on 35% of days in a forest, 30% on hills or peaks, 15% where the field has woods or rock (`BTYPE_WORLD`).
    - The draw is from the tile and the day (`hash`), never `Math.random`. Patrols, caravans and fights you start never ambush you.
    - **Caught:** no deployment, and no fleeing. Your band starts in march order, 4 files wide, on a road down the middle (`ambushLayout`).
    - **Their side:** each group stands in a wood of its own, on one flank or both (deserts, and half of hill fields: behind a low ridge), its front 84 px off the column, beyond sight. Their first charge out of hiding gets `AMBUSH`'s shock and the "Ambush!" callout. For 6 s they go straight in.
    - **Spring an ambush:** riding at a band, lord, patrol or caravan where there is cover, the panel offers it beside Fight. Your band starts hidden beside the road and theirs in march order on it, marching on west till it sees you. Neither side deploys.
    - The **ambush on the road** (only where there is cover; an encounter on the map, `world/encounters.js` `ROAD_ENC.ambush`, unseen below Scouting 2, springing as you pass): fight (the ambush above), or cut the baggage loose and run (−4 food, −15 gold, −4 morale).
  - **Lab:** `type: 'field'` (default) | `'bridge'` | `'ambush'` | `{kind, side, ford}`. `side` is the side set upon (holding the bridge, or caught on the road), `'left'` by default. A typed battle with `ground:'none'` gets `'auto'`.
  - **Balance** (`tests/visual/btypebalance.js`, mirror armies of 20 foot, 12 bows and 6 riders, seeds 1–10, each side holding): see `doc/TERRAIN_DESIGN.md` §8, "Battle types as built".
  - **Tests:** `tests/battletypes.js`.
`TERRAIN.on` is the game's switch; `tests/harness.js` turns it off, so older suites see today's field. The lab's `ground` key takes `'none'` (default), `'auto'`, `{recipe, seed, oasis, river}` (recipe `'river'`: open with a river; `river: true | {bridge, frozen, stream}`) or explicit `{hills, woods, marsh, rivers:[{x, meander, crossings:[{kind, y, w}], frozen, stream}], rocks:[{x, y, r, h}], seed}` (hills take `cliff`, `oneway`, `ramps:[degrees]`; recipe `'crags'`: hills with cliffs and mountains). Balance: the hill holder wins ~69% of mirror battles; woods cut archer damage ~27%; hill battles run ~44% longer than flat (harder climbing; `HILL.climb` is the knob). Tests: `tests/terrain.js`, `tests/concealment.js`, `tests/desertlife.js`.

## Battle feedback

**Battle events** (`battle.js`): the fight never calls the HUD or the lab. It tells what just happened with `battleEvent(type, d)` (`rout`, `rally`, `flank`, `leader`, `down`, `left`, `ambush`, `fallback`, `btype`, `power`, `ability`, `withdraw`), and whoever shows or records it listens with `battleOn(fn)`: the banners and cards (`battlehud.js`), the commander bar's cue (`commandpowers.js`), the lab's telemetry (`lab.js`). Listeners run at once, in the order they were added, and never change the fight.

**Callouts** (`callout*` in `ui/battlehud.js`, tuned by `CALLOUT`): a short side-coloured banner at the top middle of the field for the big moments: a group routs, is shattered, rallies or is flanked; a leader falls; the enemy falls back (told by `battleEvent('fallback')` from `commander.js`, heard here). Each moment is called once (the same key stays quiet for `same` combat seconds; a flank at most once per group every `flankEvery` s; arrows and steady units never trigger it). At most one new banner every `gap` real seconds, at most two on screen; a burst is cut to a short queue, minor callouts first. Your groups' routs and shatters and a leader's fall also go to the battle log; the enemy breaking is logged once at the end. No callouts in tournaments.

**Screen shake** is drawn only (`shakeView` in `gfx/vfx.js`, tuned by `SHAKE`): `frame()` offsets the view by a sine wobble, never moving `B.cam` and never drawing random numbers. A rider or big beast charging home at momentum over `SHAKE.charge` jolts the view (capped at `SHAKE.max` px, fading over `SHAKE.life` s); one of your groups routing gives a lighter jolt. `SHAKE.on=false` or the system's reduced-motion setting turns it off.

**Unit of the battle**: kills are counted per source group (`B.grpKills`, `applyDmg`); the battle report names your troop group with the most kills (ties: `resolve.damageDealt`). Not shown in tournaments, for heroes and companions, or when none of your groups fought. Tests: `tests/battlejuice.js`.

Some groups have a special order with a cooldown (`battle/unitabilities.js`, registry `UNIT_ABILITIES`). The selected groups' abilities are shown at the end of the order bar; a press (or its key) uses it for every selected group that can. A group has an ability when at least half its fighting troops qualify. Durations and cooldowns run on combat time (pausing stops them); a lit button shows the seconds left, a shaded one the seconds until ready, and the tooltip says why one is unavailable.

## Commander powers

An army's own orders from its commander (`battle/commandpowers.js`, table `COMMAND_POWERS`), apart from the groups' unit abilities and the hero's own skills (`charmind.js`). The **commander** is your hero (`B.hero`) on your side, their lord or chieftain (their first leader on the field) on theirs, else none. A power works only while the commander is alive, on the field and not running. Rally the Line and Stand Fast! reach the groups of the commander's side that have a soldier within the **command radius**: 220 px, +8% a level of Leadership, at most +60% (352 px). Leadership: your hero's `skill('lead')`; a lord's own Leadership; a chieftain's (anyone's without the skill) half his level. Cooldowns run on combat time and shrink 4% a level of Leadership, down to 60%.

| Power | Key | Effect | Cooldown (Leadership 0 → 10) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rally the Line** | Z | every routing unit of your side in range rallies through morale.js's own `rally()` (it stops, holds and re-forms; a unit rallied once and routing again is shattered, as ever) with +10 on top of the rally's 40; every wavering or Breaking unit in range gets +20 and stops breaking. A unit it rallied cannot be rallied by it again for 120 s (`cmdRallyAt`). Refused, no cooldown spent, when no one in range routs or wavers | 60 → 36 s |
| **Stand Fast!** | S | every unit of your side in range (not routing): +10 morale, 40% less morale shock, and it cannot drop to wavering (nor break) for 12 s (`standUntil` on its resolve; `BattleResolve.standing`/`standFloor`). A gold glow under its soldiers and a Stand Fast badge on its card and icon (`grpMorale(...).stand`, `statusOf` 'standfast') | 45 → 27 s |

The commander's **Scout** (O, the enemy seen through the fog for the first 10 s) was removed on the owner's call, 2026-10-20: scouting is the scout units' own Scout ahead (Unit abilities).

**The HUD:** a commander bar down the left of the field (`bhudPowers`, `#bCmd`), round gold-rimmed buttons distinct from the order bar: a sweep that empties as the cooldown runs out and the seconds; lit while Stand Fast! is on; dimmed (still pointable) with the reason in the tooltip (name, effect, cooldown, radius, Leadership, "needs your commander alive and the units within range"). Pointing at Rally or Stand Fast! draws the command radius round your hero and rings under the units it would reach (`drawCommandPowers`, render.js); a use shows it for a moment, with a ring from the commander, a word over him, a callout ("Rally!", "Stand Fast!"; theirs in their colour) and the log. Touch: the buttons are 44-46 px; a tap uses the power. Keys Z, S (in the Help's controls).

**No order:** a power goes through none of `playerOrder` (commands.js): no group's `orderSeq` moves, a character's fall-back goes on, and Auto stays as it is.

**The AI** (`powersThink`, from `commander.js` `command`; every 0.5 s of the fight): the enemy's commander always, yours under Auto. Rally the Line when a unit in range routs, or two waver; else Stand Fast! when a unit in range wavers, or as two of them (all, if fewer) come within 120 px of a foe they see. No random numbers.

**Switches:** `COMMAND_POWERS.on` (`tests/harness.js` turns it off for the older suites, so their fingerprints hold); the lab's `powers` key (true/false) overrides it, and its `commander` key `{left, right}` names a side's commander (null: none; `{group, lead}`: the first soldier of that row commands, with that Leadership). No power in tournaments. Per-side state `B.cmdp[team]` (made on first use).

**Balance** (`tests/visual/powerbalance.js`: AI-on-AI mirror armies with a commander each, Leadership 3, seeds 1-10, left/right wins): neither 5/5 (mean 75 s); both 5/5 (69 s); left only 6/4; right only 4/6 (so the side with powers wins about one battle in ten more); in the fog, both 3/7 (81 s), neither 8/2 (41 s) (measured with the commander's Scout, before its removal). Rally the Line and Stand Fast! are each used about once a battle a side. Tests: `tests/commandpowers.js`.

## Touch controls

For a phone on its side. Touch reads Pointer Events with `pointerType 'touch'` (`TOUCH` in `ui/input.js`: `hold` 450 ms, `slop` 10 CSS px, `ring` 34 CSS px); mouse, pen and keys are unchanged.
- **Battle:** tap a group to select it, tap again to send it there or onto the enemy tapped (a tap acts when the finger lifts). Hold still for about half a second (a gold ring fills; red over an enemy) to move the selection there or attack the enemy held; hold, then drag, to lay out the line as a right-drag does. Drag without holding to box groups. Two fingers: pinch to zoom about them (`camPinch`), move both to pan; a second finger cancels what the first was doing (a held line's ghost simply goes, nothing sent, the selection kept) and the two then pinch, so a pinch never selects or orders.
- **Calling a drag off (mouse and keys):** a left-click during a right-drag, or Esc, drops the line ghost: no order, the selection kept, and letting go of the right button does nothing (`dragCancel`, input.js; a left press with the right held comes as a `pointermove`, so both are read). Esc also calls off a box or a finger's press; a drag takes Esc before the window chain, so it opens no menu. Tests: `tests/dragcancel.js`.
- **Map:** tap to travel. Hold on a holding, party or site to see its card (its tooltip, "Tap:" for "Click:"); the next tap puts it away without travelling.
- **Notch and safe areas:** `viewport-fit=cover`; HUD frames, buttons, messages, banners and windows sit inside the `--e-*` edges (`--edge` plus `env(safe-area-inset-*)`); the hold card is clamped with `safeInsets()` (input.js), read from `.hudlayer`'s padding.
- **No page zoom:** the HUD is `touch-action:none` (unit cards `pan-x`, selected unit `pan-y`); windows are `pan-x pan-y`, so they scroll but never zoom; pull-to-refresh is off; on iOS `gesturestart`/`gesturechange` are cancelled.
- **Line preview on phones:** on a coarse or small screen (`GHOST` in formations.js) the dragged line is drawn in screen pixels: a 3 px gold line, 5 px slot dots and an arrowhead over a dark edge. Desktop unchanged.
- **Install:** served over http(s), `manifest.webmanifest` (fullscreen, landscape, icons `icon-192.png`/`icon-512.png`) and the `apple-*` tags let you add Ashmark to a home screen; from `file://` the manifest isn't linked.
- Tests: `tests/touch.js`, `tests/mobile.js`. Still to check on a real phone: iOS callout and page-zoom suppression, Android `pointercancel` during a pinch, notch safe areas, rotation mid-gesture, and whether 450 ms and 10 px suit real thumbs.

## Withdrawal balance

W still orders selected groups off the field, without attacking on the way: through your edge, or a flank if the enemy cuts them off (see **Field sizes and ways off the field** below; with exits off, through your edge). Withdrawal now uses the existing fatigue rules: running spends stamina, tired/spent units have their normal speed penalties, and units below `FATIGUE.runMin` can only walk. Slowing effects still apply. The hero pays from the existing hero stamina pool.

On first contact with a live, non-routing melee threat during a withdrawal order, a unit spends `WITHDRAW.disengage` (0.8 combat seconds) disengaging at `WITHDRAW.backstep` (35%) of its current fatigue/slow-adjusted movement speed. This also applies near the exit. A shooter in Melee Mode counts as a melee threat; a ranged-only or routing foe does not. The bounded phase occurs once per withdrawal order, so a close pursuer cannot reset it forever. Repeating W does not reset it; canceling withdrawal and ordering it again requires disengagement again.

Disengaging units remain vulnerable and still do not strike. An uncontested exit remains immediate; no automatic casualties or additional escape roll were added. Existing battlefield losses, campaign escape odds, rearguard losses, morale/renown penalties, allied overlap rules and routing behavior are unchanged. `tests/withdrawal.js` covers these mechanics using isolated battle fixtures; RTS, retreat settlement and flee suites cover their connection to campaign rules.

## Field sizes and ways off the field

**Five sizes** (`FIELD_SIZES` in `battle/setup.js`; the chosen one is `BFIELD`, `B.fsize`): small 960×544, big 1280×720, large 1792×1008, very large 2400×1344, huge 3200×1792 (multiples of 32 across and 16 down, so route, fog and spatial cells fit). A battle's size comes from the soldiers in it, counted from the armies before anyone spawns: under 60 small, under 150 big, under 300 large, under 600 very large, else huge. The world tile then steps it one up on open plains (open ground or road, not a swamp) and one down in forest, a mountain pass or a swamp. A held bridge stays big to large and an ambush small to big; a tournament is small. No `Math.random` is drawn. The first line of the battle names the size. The lab's `size` key (`small`, `big`, `large`, `vlarge` or `'very large'`, `huge`) forces one, sizes on or off; a sized lab may set its armies up to the field's width less 400 px apart.

**Room behind the lines:** each side deploys in a zone `back` px off its own edge, `depth` deep (`fieldZone(team)`; 32/96/256/512/832 px of open ground behind it, small to huge), and its lines form up over the field's middle band (`band`, `fieldBand`) rather than its whole height. Everything sizes itself from `BW`/`BH`: the camera (`camFit` fits the whole field, under 1 on a big one; the wheel adds 0.5 and 0.75 steps on such fields, zooming in is unchanged; the small map takes the field's shape), the route grids (a query's A* budget is a quarter of the cells on a field larger than `ROUTE.budget`'s), the fog and spatial grids, the ground and the blood layer. Big layers (ground, blood, the shooters' reach) are drawn only where the camera looks (`drawInView`), and soldiers, rocks and trees out of view are not drawn (`battleEnts`).

**Contract with the ground's generator:** `BattleSetup.start` sets `BW`, `BH` and `BFIELD` before `groundSpec`, spawning, `measureField` and `buildTerrain`; generation reads them as ever: `buildTerrain` takes the field's height from `BH` and its deployment zones from `BFIELD` (`terrain.js` `terrainZone`: `[back, back + depth]` off each edge) when the field it builds is the sized one; today's field keeps today's zones (the outer 30%). Out of a battle `BH` is today's 600 again (`fieldDone`), so the world's previews of a field read today's.

**Ways off the field** (`EXITS` in `battle/morale.js`, `B.exits`): every open stretch of all four edges is a way off: where a foot soldier may stand (deep water, rock and the field's closed rows are not), stretches under 48 px dropped. **Own side, flanks if cut off** (the owner's rule): a withdrawing or routing group (or a lone soldier) runs for its own side's edge (yours west, theirs east). It turns to a flank (top or bottom) or the far edge only when no point of home can be reached (water, rock, cliffs) or every one is cut off: an enemy group of at least a quarter of its number nearer that point than it is, or standing within 120 px across the straight way there (`exitCut`, `cutBy`, `across`); one chasing from behind cuts nothing off. Among the points still allowed it takes the one, every 48 px, that costs least: the length of its way there (`routeDist` round cliffs, rock and water; straight where nothing blocks; none it cannot reach), dearer toward the enemy and cheaper away from them (`away`), plus its danger, the enemy its straight line passes within 200 px of (`danger`, `weight`), plus the chase, any enemy group with less than a 400 px lead on that point (`lead`, `chase`), each by its strength against the group's own. It is chosen when the group starts to run, looked at again every 2 s and kept unless another costs under 80% of it. Each man makes for the nearest point of that stretch within 120 px of the chosen one. Your withdraw order follows the same rule at once (the order line points there); so do the enemy's captains. In AI-vs-AI lab battles at every size (fog on and off) about 97% of those who break run for their own edge; the rest were cut off. The field shows a faint strip along every usable stretch, blue where your men head (with **Withdraw**), faintly red where theirs run. Those who get away are counted as before, whichever edge they took (`u.left`, `B.withdrawn`, `B.fledFoes`).

**Switches:** `FIELD_SIZES.on` and `EXITS.on` (both on in the game); `tests/harness.js` turns them off so the older suites keep today's field and the two edges. Tests: `tests/fieldsize.js` (choice, lab key, zones, camera and small map, grids, painting a huge field, exits and blocked edges, a rout and a withdrawal, the escaped counted, an AI battle at each size to the end, timings at 400 and 800 soldiers).

**Cost** (headless, no GPU): simulation 2.7–3.3 ms/frame at 400 soldiers and 7–9 ms at 800 at every size; drawing the whole field 9–10 ms at 400 and 17–23 ms at 800 (zoomed in, less). A huge field paints its ground in about 0.35 s (arena) + 0.15–0.25 s (terrain); each full-field canvas is 22 MB there (arena, ground, blood; the shooters' reach when shown).

## Developer console and battle laboratory

Click **DEV** (top right), press **F2**, or press the physical backquote key to open developer tools. Escape closes the window; simulation stops while it is open. Up/Down recalls command history. Tools work from the title screen, world map and battle; teleporting requires the unobstructed world map.

Campaign cheats affect live state and can persist in normal saves. Keep a separate save if you want to undo them. These are explicit commands, not arbitrary JavaScript.

| Command | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| `money 1000` / `money -100` | Add/subtract gold without allowing a negative balance. |
| `where` | Show world-pixel and tile coordinates. |
| `tp 1200 800` / `tp tile 50 60` | Teleport to walkable map coordinates; cancel previous travel/chase. |
| `goto Ashford` | Teleport near a holding; names containing spaces need no quoting. |
| `get player` / `get player.attr` | Inspect campaign state and discover fields. |
| `set player.attr.agi 12` | Edit an existing scalar field. Values use JSON syntax, including quotes for strings. |
| `get game.day` / `set game.day 30` | Inspect state; game writes are limited to day, clock, speed and paused. |
| `units bow` | List matching unit definition IDs. |
| `roster` | List current battle unit IDs, sides, health and positions. |
| `get unit.1` / `set unit.1.s.spd 80` | Inspect a live fighter or override its stats; use IDs from `roster`. |
| `battle hum_bow 5 hum_foot 5` | Start a paused isolated matchup; `battle` alone opens the selection form. |
| `pause`, `step 60`, `restart`, `exit` | Control a battle test. Step advances 1–600 frames at 1/60 second per frame and pauses afterward. |
| `help`, `clear` | Show command examples or clear console output. |

Player edits do not rebuild an already-spawned hero's battlefield stats; edit `unit.<id>.s` to change live stats. Writes cannot replace objects, create arbitrary fields, traverse prototypes, change unit identity/group links, or edit linked shared Unit/campaign objects. Stat edits copy the live stats first so they do not alter the shared unit definitions. Extreme but finite developer values can still invalidate gameplay assumptions; these are debugging tools, not validated progression commands.

The **Battle test** tab builds two armies, each with **1–10 independent groups**, including optional characters. Duplicate troop types stay separate. Add/remove/reorder rows, set counts, names, formations and files (0 = auto). Each troop group permits 1–100 fighters, with a performance limit of **200 fighters per army**. Wedge requires at least six cavalry; formations must fit their field/starting zone. The builder rejects unsuitable formations instead of silently changing them.

Your current hero may occupy one left-side group. Companion choices are independent level-1 templates on either side, with one copy of each companion per army; they do not use or injure your campaign recruits. Characters require count 1. Troop-only tests have no extra fighting hero and do not inherit campaign troop talent/perk bonuses. Both sides start from their own unit kind's base morale. Hero tests use your current build/equipment, including its normal effects on the left army.

Choose terrain, climate, seed, player-side AI, initial pause and morale/routing. **Deploy left army before battle** freezes the field and uses the normal right-click/right-drag placement controls, independently of the campaign deployment preference. The right army deploys automatically in its chosen formations. **Begin** ends deployment; if Start paused is checked, the battle remains paused for stepping. Separation applies when deployment is skipped. The existing `battle hum_bow 5 hum_foot 5` command remains a quick one-group matchup without deployment.

Built-in presets include combined arms, duplicate formations, mounted archers vs footmen, and footmen vs berserkers. Save/load up to 20 named user presets in the separate `ashmark.lab.presets.v1` browser-storage key. Save / replace overwrites only the named user preset; built-ins are read-only. Delete affects only the selected user preset. **Use last test** copies the last successful configuration into the draft. Editing the draft or live stats does not change the configuration used by **Restart**.

The persistent test toolbar offers Play/Pause, Step, Restart, Exit, Tools and **Report**. The report compares each configured group on both sides: surviving, remaining-on-field and dead counts, kills, effective damage dealt/taken, morale samples (recent six shown, older samples in JSON), and route/withdrawal/field-exit timestamps. Routed exits and ordered withdrawals have separate counts and timestamps. Effective damage is post-mitigation HP removed, capped to the target's HP remaining; dodges and overkill do not count. Damage without a configured source is explicitly uncredited. The downloadable JSON includes the normalized configuration and seed, bounded event/morale histories with truncation counts, and a plain final unit snapshot. Survivor counts include living units that exited; kills count lethal damage only. Normal selection, move, attack, facing, speed and formation controls remain available. Escape and the HUD menu open the test tools. Starting a test is allowed from the title screen or unobstructed world map, never over a campaign battle or unresolved choice.

The laboratory swaps `P`/`G` contents for private copies while preserving their record identities, and restores the original nested records, screen and random function on exit. World simulation is not advanced. Saving/loading is blocked during a test; completion freezes the field instead of running campaign settlement, so test rewards, casualties, hero training, ammo and gold edits are discarded. Restart restores the campaign then recreates the same configuration. Seed initializes the existing random stream; exact simulation reproducibility still depends on matching frame/input/render activity.

Implementation: `js/ui/developer.js`, `js/battle/lab.js`; the normal setup and simulation remain in use. `tests/developer.js` exercises commands, real form/keyboard/toolbar events, private stat edits, natural battle completion, restart and restoration, and save/load isolation. `tests/armylab.js` covers independent armies, character templates, validation, deployment, maximum-group setups, restart, presets and actual builder controls. `tests/labdiagnostics.js` covers isolated damage attribution, group metrics, route/withdraw events, morale samples, bounded history and JSON serialization. `tests/visual/developer.js` prepares a desktop/narrow preview (`&tab=console` selects the console); `tests/visual/armylab.js` previews a mixed-army preset and its deployed field.

## Running tests

Each suite in `tests/` runs headless in Chrome or Edge by opening `Ashmark.html?test=<suite>`. `tests/harness.js` loads `tests/kit.js` first, the shared helpers (`Kit.suite`, `Kit.begin`, `Kit.world`, `Kit.done`, `Kit.goHome`, `Kit.travel`, `Kit.frames`, `Kit.stub`/`rolls`/`seeded`, `Kit.stubStream(name, v, fn)` (a named stream's draws fixed, as `Kit.stub` fixes Math.random's), `Kit.meet`, `Kit.panel`/`text`, `Kit.q`/`qa`, `Kit.key`, `Kit.storyKinds`: the test thread and gathering kinds, registered in the test page only); it is not a suite, and the sweep leaves it out. Suites on it so far: `relations`, `reputation`, `leaders`, `quests`, `monsters`, `coreparts`, `story`, `kinds`, `threads`, `streams` (S4: a test-only encounter kind and a changed weight leave the world, the shared Math.random and a battle as they were; the streams saved, loaded and started afresh; the estates' sort keys; the house order's line). Windows (PowerShell):

```
.\tests\run-tests.ps1                                  # every suite (about 4 min)
.\tests\run-tests.ps1 -Parallel 4                      # every suite, 4 browsers at once (about 1.5 min)
.\tests\run-tests.ps1 -Suites rosters,parties,garrison # selected suites
.\tests\run-tests.ps1 -Suites siege -Seed (1..8)       # reproducible random scenarios
```

From bash: `bash tests/run-tests.sh [-j N] [-s SEED] [suite ...]`. On Windows it hands over to the PowerShell runner; elsewhere (or with `ASHMARK_DUMP_DOM=1`) it runs a `--dump-dom` browser per suite.

After each suite the runner prints `# N pass, N fail, N.N s`, and at the end `# summary: ...` and `# failing: ...`; it exits non-zero if any line starts with `FAIL`, `EXC` or `ERR` (every line is counted, not just a suite's first). Each worker reuses a locked profile slot under `%TEMP%shmark-tests` (a new Chrome profile costs 10-20 s before its first page) and clears its stored data first; it never attaches to a user browser. `-Browser` picks another Chrome/Edge, `-TimeoutSeconds` the per-suite deadline. Seeded runs reset the random stream just before `RUN()`; a page can also be opened as `?test=siege&seed=1`.

`tests/visual/` holds pages run by name, not in the default sweep: screenshot scenes (`visual/shot`, `visual/<feature>`) and reports that never fail: `visual/perf` (hot-path timings), `visual/worldsim` (five 120-day campaigns), `visual/monsterbalance`, `visual/dragonbalance`, `visual/matchupbalance` (BAL lines), `visual/moraleaudit` and `visual/confidenceaudit` (morale diagnostics), `visual/powerbalance` (commander powers, AI on AI). `mapview` also draws the whole map over the page, so `Ashmark.html?test=mapview` is a quick way to look at it.

With `-Gen` every suite runs on the generated classic world (`gen=1`, doc/WORLD_DESIGN.md §15.2); the suites read places, never today's coordinates (a battle fingerprint's fixture stands on today's ground; the outcomes and the world battle that read the world round them keep a second set of constants for it, recorded with G1–G8). The suite list with `-Gen` is clear (2026-10-22). `tests/worldsanity.js` holds §8.14's Phase 2 rules on 8 Normal and 2 Large seeds, each world made as a new game makes it: every holding and camp reachable from every capital, also with the seasonal crossings shut; each capital's liking and land; capitals and realms balanced; a fair start for each race; ports on water joined to the sea or a port; rivers never uphill, ending in water, no great river freezing, crossings at most 60 tiles apart; each climate 6% of the land (no emptiness, wild land, the lair and the shape metrics, later phases', as INFO). Reports for the generated world: `visual/worldsanity` (the same over 30 and 10 seeds), `visual/worldscale` (per preset: generation by stage and the typed arrays it makes, putting in, a chunk painted, the router, `updateWorld`, the day's jobs, the save plain and gzipped), `visual/loadtimes` (each wait, per size) and `visual/btypebalance` (Hold the bridge's share of the world's fights).

## Art

Everything is drawn by code (`js/gfx/sprites.js`, `js/gfx/worldart.js`); nothing is loaded from `graphics/`. Buildings on the world map come from the PX engine in `../engine` (see its README and `gallery.html`), and so do its woods, hills, mountain ranges and small things (`worldnature.js`, `gallery.html?view=nature`). Every holding is a set of placed buildings, each on its own tiles of the grid (`js/world/lots.js`): its centre (keep, citadel or hall, whose walls are the holding's fortification: Fortify upgrades it), houses for its people, the buildings of its estates, and fields, pastures and pits flat on the tiles they keep. Realms grow their holdings over time; in your fiefs you place what you build, and it goes up behind scaffolding. Flags are their own animated sprite.

The generated world (`?gen=1`) has its own water and roads (`worldart.js`, doc/WORLD_DESIGN.md §12.1): water rounded at its corners and shaded by width, banks with reeds and stones, fords with stepping stones, stone and wooden bridges, ferries, rapids, brooks along the flow (painted by `worldnature.js`), sand rims and shallows on the coasts, and roads painted along their smoothed lines at any angle. `tests/visual/rivers.js` shows every piece for approval (a page of crops: screenshot it at 1400 x 2200); today's map is painted as before.

After the owner's play-test (2026-10-22): every road's end at a holding is led round its centre (`GENART.yard`) and up into its gate, over worn ground (`gateLead`); a crossing is met straight on its axis and a bridge's deck by a ramp of road as wide as it (`straightOn`, `offDeck`); a fork's end is carried onto the way it joins (`joinEnds`); a ford is a band of rippled shallows fading from the river's colour, rounded into gravel on each bank, its stepping stones scattered along it (`fordAt`). On both maps the frost's snow is laid by its cover a tile (`snowCover`, `FROST`: how deep in the frost, how cold below the frost line on the generated world, how high), rising over the frost's edge and thinning out in patches over the grass beside it (`landRGB`), and the swamp's open ground stands with sparse low mangroves (`engine/worldnature.js` `FEN`). `tests/visual/worldart.js` shows each of these in the game's own view for approval (screenshot it at 1200 x 2340, `&gen=1` or not).

To look at the result, take a screenshot of a prepared scene (`scene` is `world` or `battle`; `town`, `mount` and `zoom` are optional):

## Sound

Everything heard is synthesised by WebAudio as it plays (`js/core/audio.js`, `js/core/music.js`): oscillators, filtered noise and envelopes, no files (fetching is unreliable from `file://`, and a phone downloads nothing). One `AudioContext`, made or woken by the first key, click or touch (the autoplay rule), suspended while the tab is hidden. Each sound and each mood feeds the effects or the music bus, both into master, then a compressor and a soft clip, so a big melee never clips.
- **Effects:** `sfx(name, {vol, pan, pitch, delay})` plays a cue of `SFX` (each cue: a small recipe, `gap` its rate limit, `max` how many at once, `pri` who gives way at the cap); `sfxAt(name, x, y)` plays one heard from a point of the field: panned, quieter away from the middle of the battle camera, unheard three half-views off. At most `AUDIO.voices` (24) sound at once; past it a more important cue takes the oldest lesser one's place, else it is dropped. Cues: UI `click confirm back refused coin levelup notify`; world `encounter town day`; battle `horn charge clash hit block loose arrowhit brace gallop rout rally victory defeat`.
- **Music:** `music(mood)`: `menu`, `world`, `town`, `battle` (Dorian, Mixolydian, Aeolian; lute or harp, drone, recorder or fiddle, frame drum and tabor) and the stingers `victory` and `defeat`. Each mood draws three motifs from its seed; each four-bar phrase is made anew from them (repeated, moved, inverted, a note split, a new cadence and chord walk), so it is recognisable but never loops. A lookahead scheduler puts notes on the audio clock 0.4 s ahead; a new mood crossfades over 1.8 s; a stinger plays out before the next mood comes in.
- **The game:** `audioWatch()` (main.js's loop) sets the mood from the scene (title, road, town window or streets, battle, its stinger once over; the Esc menu keeps what is under it) and plays the encounter alert, a town's gate and bells, the day's chime, level-up and the horn as a fight begins. Every button (one listener in ui.js) clicks, confirms (primary), goes back (a bottom bar), chinks coin (gold changed) or refuses (a red message).
- **The battle:** each hook asks `sfxOn()` first, so with sound off it reckons nothing. Blows and missiles (attacks.js `Fighter.hit`, projectiles.js `Missile.strike`; `blowSound`): on a shield's front `block` (the test vfxHit splinters by), an arrow or bolt `arrowhit`, steel on mail or plate `clash`, magic, beasts and the rest `hit`; a ranged troop's shot `loose` (attacks.js `Fighter.attack`, not magic); an arrow into the ground a soft `arrowhit`. Only one in `SND_EVERY` of each kind is played (a counter, never a dice), then the cues' `gap` and `max` (the blows, shots and strikes at most 16 at once) and the voice cap. morale.js: `rout` as a group routs (`route`), `rally` as it rallies; unitabilities.js: each ability's `snd` (Brace `brace`, Spur `gallop`, Rally `rally`); commands.js `setOrder`: your charge order, `gallop` with riders in the selection, else `charge`; commander.js: `gallop` as AI riders set off on a charge; battlehud.js `calloutAmbush`: `charge` from the ambushers.
- **Settings:** Settings → Sound: on/off and three volumes (`PREFS` `mute`, `volmaster`, `volmusic`, `volsfx`, stored as `ashmark.<key>`). **Shift+M** mutes anywhere (M opens the full map).
- **Off:** `AUDIO.on=false` (`tests/harness.js` sets it) or no WebAudio: every call is a cheap no-op that never throws. Sound never touches `Math.random` (own `mkRng`), so seeded battles are unchanged.
- **Gallery:** `sounds.html` plays every cue (battle cues also at the left and right edge), each mood and stinger, has the volume sliders, a voice-cap box, a peak meter and a battle-stress button (60 random battle cues in 2 s). Tests: `tests/audio.js` (offline rendering: every cue and mood heard, never past full scale; limits; settings; the mood following the scene; a lab battle heard, under the cap, and fought the same with sound on or off).

## The screen
One screen, filling the window at a whole-number scale (F11 or the corner button for full screen). Everything lies on it: your
band's card top left (your hero and level; the band's stamina and health bars, its tags; gold, food and drink in days, troops,
power, morale, pace; what runs short is red: see Supplies), the minimap top right, a short hint top middle, messages bottom left
(they fade; L shows the whole log), the dock bottom right (Camp Z, Hero C, Skills K, Bag I, Log L, People P, Help H, zoom, save,
full screen, menu), battle orders bottom middle in a battle. Hold the mouse or a finger on the map for the action ring.
Hover anything on the map for what it is, its strength against you and what a click does. Fog of war (js/world/fog.js) keeps
what you have explored.

**The full map** (`ui/map.js`; doc/WORLD_DESIGN.md §3.4, §12.4): **M**, or a click or a tap on the minimap, opens the whole world
in a window (header with the world's name, size, how much you have explored and − + for the zoom; the map beside its legend;
Close). M again or Esc closes it; the world waits meanwhile. The picture is made from the tile data (`fullMapPic`: the minimap's
colours, rivers by width, each realm's tint and a stronger line where two realms meet, the dark over land never seen); the
holdings (by kind, named from town up, villages from 2 px a tile), the crossings on land you have seen and you are marks over it.
It opens at the largest scale that fits, never under 1 px a tile (1 px per 2 tiles on a map wider than 640: Huge), so Normal
shows at 1 px a tile, Large scrolls and Huge is 512 × 384; + − step it, a click zooms in where it lands (1:1 by region at
Huge), a drag pans. On a phone on its side the legend slims to a column and the map scrolls. Tests: `tests/fullmap.js`.

**The New world screen** (`ui/worldsetup.js`; doc/WORLD_DESIGN.md §4, Phase 2b; with `?gen=1`): after your race and past (a
past's card names `world:<past>`, `session.js` `begin`), the world's settings, then "Forging the world…". Left (it scrolls):
the presets (Classic, Normal, Large, Huge, Wild Frontier, Crowded Realms; touching a setting makes it Custom, and a mix that is a
preset's again shows as that preset), the seed (typed, Random; a world code typed or pasted there sets everything), the world code
with Copy (`worldsettings.js` `worldCode`, `parseWorldCode`: `ASH1-<preset>-<every setting and the size, base 36>-<seed>`), the
size (or Custom: width and height with − and +), the holdings (Fewer, Normal, More: `HOLDING_DENSITY` times the area's default,
27 / 40 / 66 at Normal / Large / Huge, More today's 37 / 55 / 90; or a number with − and +) and the Advanced fold-out (the rest,
those later phases read marked later). Classic locks everything that changes the map; monsters, bandits and your start stay open.
Every change is clamped at once, with a note when a limit moved a value. Right: the preview, your start city ringed: the quick
sketch (`worldgen.js` `genSketch`: the real land field and climate noise at 256 points across, mountains, rivers and holdings
approximate; about 70 ms at any size), redrawn 200 ms after a change, or the real map when it is made (the classic world the

Regenerate, the counts and the time it takes to forge here (measured once, kept in `localStorage`). A generated world but
